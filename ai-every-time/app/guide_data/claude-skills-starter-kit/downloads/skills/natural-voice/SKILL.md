# Natural voice

Edit for a clear, human voice: writing people want to read. Keep the meaning, the facts and the writer's intent.

This skill improves writing. It is not for passing off AI-written work as someone's own where that breaks the rules, such as in coursework or exams. If that seems to be the goal, say so and decline.

## Before you edit

Find out, or infer from the text:
- who will read it and what they should do or feel afterwards;
- the channel (email, report, post, speech);
- whose voice to match. If the person shares a sample of their own writing, match its rhythm, vocabulary and punctuation.

## What to cut

- **Stock openings and endings**: "In today's fast-paced world", "Let's dive in", "In conclusion", "I hope this helps".
- **Inflated words**: delve, leverage (as a verb), utilize, robust, seamless, tapestry, testament, pivotal, realm, unlock, elevate, empower, "navigate the landscape".
- **Throat-clearing**: "It's important to note that", "It's worth mentioning that".
- **Padding in threes**: lists of three where one or two items carry the point.
- **Formatting a short text doesn't need**: headings, bold and bullet points in a few paragraphs. Turn them back into prose.
- **Dash chains**: long dashes used as all-purpose punctuation. Use commas, colons or full stops.
- **Flattery and filler**: "Great question!", "Absolutely!", summaries of what was just said.
- **Repeated sentence shapes**: "It's not X, it's Y" more than once, or every paragraph ending on a neat one-liner.

## What to add

- Specifics: the number, the name, the example, the actual claim.
- Variety: mix short sentences with longer ones.
- A point of view: say what the writer thinks and why.
- Plain verbs: "use", "help", "show", "start".

## How to work

1. Read the whole draft once. Mark the problems.
2. Rewrite in one pass.
3. Keep every fact, figure, name, date and quotation exactly as given. Never add new facts. If something looks wrong, flag it instead of fixing it silently.
4. Keep roughly the same length unless asked to shorten it.
5. Return the rewrite first, then three to five bullet points on the main changes.
6. Offer one alternative: more casual or more formal.

## Check before you return it

- [ ] Would the intended reader believe a person wrote this?
- [ ] Is every fact from the original still there and still right?
- [ ] Is there at least one concrete detail per paragraph?
- [ ] Did you remove more than you added?
