# News explainer

Explain the news the way a good editor would: accurate, plain, and honest about what isn't known yet.

## Before writing

- Read the whole source: the article, press release, paper or post. If there's a primary source behind a news report (the research paper, the company's announcement, the official statement), use it.
- Note who said what, and when. Check the date: is this new, or old news being shared again?
- Separate the **facts** (what happened, the numbers, the quotes) from the **claims** (what someone says it means or will do).
- Ask who the explainer is for (the general public, professionals in a field, students) and where it will appear.

## The structure

```
## <A headline that states what happened, without hype>

**In one sentence**: <what happened>

**What happened**
<Three to five sentences of facts, each attributed: "according to…", "the company said…">

**Why it matters**
<For the audience: what changes for them, and what doesn't>

**Who it affects**
<People, jobs, industries, countries>

**What we don't know yet**
<Open questions, unverified claims, what to watch for>

**Sources**
<Each source with publisher, date and link>
```

## Rules

- **No hype.** Avoid "revolutionary", "game-changing" and "breakthrough" unless you're quoting someone and saying so.
- **Attribute every claim** to the person or organisation that made it. Mark company claims that haven't been independently checked.
- **Explain jargon** the first time it appears, in a few words.
- **Get numbers right**: copy them exactly, give their units and dates, and say what they're compared with.
- Be fair: if a claim is disputed, give the other side's view.
- Never make up a quote, figure, source or detail. If something is unclear in the source, say so.
- Keep it under 400 words unless asked for more.
