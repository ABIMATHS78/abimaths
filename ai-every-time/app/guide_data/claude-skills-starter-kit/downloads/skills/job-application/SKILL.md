# Job application

Help the person show, truthfully, why they fit this job.

## 1. Read the job ad

Pull out:
- the must-have skills and experience;
- the nice-to-haves;
- the words the employer uses for them (match those words where they're true for the person);
- what the role is really for: the problems the person hired will solve.

Show this as a short list, and ask if anything important is missing.

## 2. Match the evidence

Ask for the current CV, and go through the must-haves one by one:
- Where does the person's experience show this? Find the best example.
- Can the example show a result? Numbers help: time saved, money raised, people trained, error rates cut.
- Where there's a real gap, say so, and suggest how to address it honestly (a course, a project, transferable experience).

## 3. Tailor the CV

- A three-line profile at the top, written for this role.
- Experience in reverse date order; under each role, three to five bullet points starting with a strong verb and ending with a result where possible.
- Put the most relevant experience and skills first.
- Use plain headings ("Experience", "Education", "Skills") and a simple layout without text boxes or tables, which some application systems read badly.
- Usually one to two pages.

## 4. Write the cover letter

Under 350 words:
1. The role, and one specific reason they want it at this organisation.
2. Two or three examples that match the must-haves, with results.
3. What they'd bring in the first months.
4. A short, confident close.

## Rules

- **Never invent or exaggerate** experience, titles, dates, qualifications or results. If something can't be supported, leave it out.
- Keep personal details to what's needed: no date of birth, photo or ID numbers unless the person says that's normal where they're applying.
- Use the spelling and conventions of the country of the job, such as "CV" or "résumé".
- Return the tailored CV, the cover letter, and a list of the changes made.
