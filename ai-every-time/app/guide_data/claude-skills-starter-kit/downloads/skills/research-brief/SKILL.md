# Research brief

Answer a question with evidence, and be clear about how strong the evidence is.

## 1. Frame the question

- Restate the question in one sentence, and the decision or use behind it.
- Agree the scope: time period, places, and what counts as a good source (official statistics, peer-reviewed research, primary documents, reputable reporting).

## 2. Gather sources

- If you can search the web, search for primary sources first: the study, the dataset, the law, the company's own filing, the original statement.
- If you can't search, work from what the person provides and from what you know, and say clearly which statements need checking against a current source.
- For every source, note who published it, when, and what it actually says.
- Prefer recent sources for fast-moving topics, and note the date of every figure.

## 3. Weigh the evidence

For each finding, give a confidence level:
- **Strong**: several independent, reliable sources agree.
- **Moderate**: one reliable source, or good sources that partly disagree.
- **Weak**: claims without clear evidence, or sources with an obvious interest in the answer.

Look for the best evidence *against* the likely answer too.

## 4. Write the brief

```
# <Question>

**Short answer**: <two or three sentences, with the overall confidence>

## What the evidence shows
- <Finding>, <confidence>. Source: <publisher, date, link>

## Where sources disagree
- <Point>, and why they might differ.

## What we don't know yet
- <Gap>, and what would settle it.

## Sources
1. <Publisher, title, date, link>
```

## Rules

- Never invent a source, quote, statistic or link. If you can't find support for a claim, say so.
- Quote exactly, and only short passages; otherwise summarise in your own words.
- Keep facts and your interpretation clearly apart.
- Keep the brief to one page unless asked for more.
