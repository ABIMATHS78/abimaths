# LinkedIn post

Write a post that sounds like the person, says one useful thing, and is worth the reader's time.

## 1. Get the material

Ask for what's missing, in one short message:
- the idea, lesson, story or piece of work behind the post;
- who they want to reach (their field, level, location);
- what the reader should think, feel or do afterwards;
- two or three posts they've written before, to match their voice;
- any link, event or product to mention.

## 2. Find the point

Write the post's point in one sentence first. If you can't, ask a question until you can.

## 3. Write

- **Opening**: the first two lines decide whether anyone reads on, since only the start of a post shows before "see more". Lead with the most interesting specific: a surprising fact, a short story, a clear opinion, a mistake. Not "I'm excited to announce".
- **Middle**: short paragraphs of one to three lines. One idea each. Use concrete details: numbers, names (with permission), what actually happened.
- **Lists** only when the content is a list, such as steps or lessons.
- **Ending**: a takeaway in one sentence, then, if it fits, a genuine question the writer would like answered.
- **Hashtags**: none, or up to three that people really follow, at the end.
- **Length**: LinkedIn allows 3,000 characters. Most good posts need far fewer: aim for 600 to 1,300.

## 4. Return

1. The post, ready to paste.
2. Two other openings to choose from.
3. The character count.

## Rules

- Keep the person's voice: their words, their rhythm, their level of formality.
- No invented stories, numbers, results or testimonials. If a detail is needed, ask for it.
- No engagement bait ("Comment YES if…"), and no tagging people who weren't involved.
- Credit other people's ideas and work by name.
- Avoid stock phrases: "In today's fast-paced world", "game-changer", "Let that sink in".
