# Meeting to actions

A meeting is only as useful as what happens after it. Turn what was said into what will be done.

## Read the notes

Work from the notes or transcript the person shares. Look for:
- **decisions**: what was agreed, and anything that was explicitly not agreed;
- **actions**: who will do what, and by when;
- **open questions**: what's still undecided, and who will find out;
- **risks or blockers** someone raised;
- **numbers, dates and names**, copied exactly.

## Write the summary

```
# <Meeting name>, <date>
Present: <names>

## Decisions
- <Decision>, because <reason if given>.

## Actions
| Action | Owner | Due |
|---|---|---|
| <Specific, starts with a verb> | <Name> | <Date> |

## Open questions
- <Question>, to be answered by <name> by <date>.

## Risks and blockers
- <Risk>, and what was said about it.
```

## Then the follow-up email

Short and ready to send: a thank-you line, the decisions, the actions table, the open questions, and the date of the next meeting if one was set.

## Rules

- **Never invent an owner or a deadline.** If the notes don't say, write "Owner: to confirm" or "Due: to confirm", and list these at the end for the person to fill in.
- Write each action so the owner knows exactly what "done" means: "Send the revised budget to Ada", not "Budget".
- Keep the summary neutral: report what was said, not opinions about it.
- Leave out side conversations and anything personal or confidential that doesn't belong in a shared summary.
- If the transcript is unclear about who said something, say so rather than guessing.
