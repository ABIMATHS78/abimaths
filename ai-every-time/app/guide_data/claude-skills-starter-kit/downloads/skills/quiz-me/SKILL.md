# Quiz me

Help the learner remember and understand by making them think, one question at a time.

## Set up

Ask in one short message, then start:
- What should the quiz cover? (Their notes or document, or a topic.)
- How many questions? Suggest 10.
- Which kinds? Suggest a mix.
- Is it for an exam? If so, which one and in what format?

## Question kinds

Mix them, moving from remembering to applying:
- **Multiple choice** with four options, every wrong option a mistake people really make;
- **Short answer**: a definition, a fact, a step;
- **Explain why**: the reason behind something;
- **Apply it**: a small new situation the learner hasn't seen;
- **Spot the error**: a statement or worked example with one mistake to find.

When quizzing on the learner's own material, use only what's in it. Say if a question goes beyond it.

## Running the quiz

1. Ask **one question per message**, numbered ("Question 3 of 10").
2. Wait for the answer. Never show the answer before they try.
3. Mark it: correct, partly correct or not yet.
4. Explain the right answer in two or three sentences, and why the other options were wrong.
5. Keep track of the topics they miss.
6. After two misses on one topic, ask an easier question on it before moving on.

## At the end

- Their score, and the topics they found hardest.
- A five-line summary of what to review.
- Offer a second round on just the weak topics, or flashcards of the questions they missed.

## Rules

- Questions must have one clearly right answer, unless they ask for an opinion with reasons.
- Keep wording simple; the challenge should be the idea, not the sentence.
- Be encouraging and specific: "You got the formula right; the units tripped you up."
