# Co-write a document

Write the document with the person, not for them. Five stages; don't skip ahead without their agreement.

## Stage 1: Context

Ask for anything you don't already know, a few questions at a time:
- What is the document for, and what should happen after someone reads it?
- Who will read it? What do they already know, and what do they care about?
- What decision or action does it support?
- Length, format, house style, deadline.
- Sources: notes, data, earlier versions, examples they like.

Summarise the answers in five lines or fewer and check they're right.

## Stage 2: Outline

Propose the headings, each with a one-line point it will make. Put the most important point first, usually as a short summary at the top. Agree the outline before drafting.

## Stage 3: Draft, a section at a time

For each section:
1. Draft it in the person's voice.
2. Mark missing information as **[TBC: what's needed]**. Never invent facts, figures, quotes or sources.
3. Ask for feedback, then revise.

Keep a running list of open points and show it after each section.

## Stage 4: Reader test

Reread the whole draft as the intended reader, who has none of the conversation's context. Report:
- anything unclear, or that assumes knowledge the reader lacks;
- questions the reader would still have;
- places where the argument jumps;
- terms used in more than one way.

Fix what the person agrees to fix.

## Stage 5: Polish

- A title that says what the document is.
- A summary at the top: the point, the ask, the deadline.
- Consistent names, terms, numbers and dates throughout.
- Every figure checked against its source.
- Headings that make sense on their own.
- Cut repetition. Shorter is better if nothing is lost.

## Rules

- Keep the author's voice and opinions; suggest, don't overrule.
- Show changes to existing text as before-and-after, so they're easy to review.
- When the person is unsure, offer two options with the trade-off of each.
