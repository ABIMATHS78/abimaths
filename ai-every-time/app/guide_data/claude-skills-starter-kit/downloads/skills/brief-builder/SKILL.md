# Brief builder

People think out loud. Turn what they said into a brief that anyone, human or AI, could act on.

## Steps

1. **Read everything once** without acting on it. Notes, transcripts and pasted fragments all count.
2. **Pull out the pieces**:
   - the goal (why this matters);
   - the deliverable (what exactly gets made);
   - requirements (must-haves);
   - preferences (nice-to-haves);
   - limits: deadline, budget, length, tools, format;
   - audience;
   - examples or references mentioned;
   - anything they said to avoid.
3. **Resolve contradictions.** If two statements clash, list both and ask which wins.
4. **Fill gaps with at most three questions**, the ones whose answers change the work most. For anything else, make a sensible assumption and label it.
5. **Write the brief** in the format below.
6. **Confirm**: "Is this right? Anything to change before I start?" Don't start the work until they agree.

## Brief format

```
## Task brief: <short title>

**Goal**: <one sentence on the outcome and why it matters>
**Deliverable**: <what will be produced, in what format>
**Audience**: <who it's for>

**Requirements**
- <must-have>

**Preferences**
- <nice-to-have>

**Limits**: <deadline · length · tools · budget>
**Avoid**: <things they don't want>
**Assumptions**: <what you assumed, so they can correct it>

**Steps**
1. <step>
2. <step>

**Done when**: <checkable criteria, such as "a 2-page PDF, approved by Ada, sent by Friday">
```

## Rules

- Use the person's own words for key terms, so nothing gets lost in translation.
- Keep it short: a brief is usually under 250 words.
- Don't add requirements they didn't ask for. Suggestions go under a separate "Ideas" line, clearly marked.
- If they want to hand the brief to another AI tool, offer a version written as a prompt: role, context, task, format, constraints, and examples.
