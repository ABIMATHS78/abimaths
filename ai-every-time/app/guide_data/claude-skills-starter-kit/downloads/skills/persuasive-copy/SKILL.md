# Persuasive copy

Write copy that gets one specific reader to take one specific action, honestly.

## 1. Get the brief

Ask for whatever is missing, in one short message:
- **Product or offer**: what it is, the price, what's included.
- **Reader**: who they are, what they want, what frustrates them now.
- **The one action**: sign up, buy, book a call, reply.
- **Proof**: results, numbers, testimonials or reviews you're allowed to use, awards, guarantees.
- **Objections**: why a reader might say no.
- **Voice**: three words for the brand's tone, plus any words to avoid.
- **Channel**: landing page, email, ad, social post, and any length limits.

## 2. Find the angle

Write one sentence for each, then pick the strongest:
- the outcome the reader wants;
- the pain they want gone;
- what's new or different;
- the proof that it works;
- the common belief it challenges.

## 3. Write

**Headlines**: write ten across the angles above, then pick the best three and say why. Strong headlines are specific, useful and clear before clever. Read each one aloud.

**Body**: use the framework that fits:
- **PAS** (problem, agitate, solve) for pain-driven offers;
- **AIDA** (attention, interest, desire, action) for landing pages;
- **Before, after, bridge** for transformations.

Turn features into benefits with "so that": "Daily reminders, *so that* you keep your streak."

**Call to action**: start with a verb, say what happens next, and reduce the risk: "Start your free week. Cancel any time."

## 4. Return

1. The recommended version, ready to paste.
2. Two variants to test, each changing one thing (headline, angle or call to action), with what each tests.
3. A one-line note on the reader and angle you chose.

## Rules

- **No false or unprovable claims**, invented numbers, fake reviews, fake scarcity or fake deadlines.
- Respect the channel's length limits; if unsure, ask.
- Health, money, legal and children's products have extra advertising rules. Keep claims modest and suggest a compliance check.
- Use the reader's words, not industry jargon.
- One idea per sentence. Short paragraphs.
