# Safety incident report

Write a clear, factual record of a patient-safety incident that helps the organisation learn and stop it happening again. Describe events and systems, and blame no one.

## Ask first

If any of this is missing, ask for it in one short message:
- what happened, in the reporter's own words, with dates and times;
- where it happened, and the roles of the people involved, not their names;
- what was done straight away, and the patient's condition now, as far as the reporter knows;
- the organisation's report form or headings, and its harm categories, if it has them.

If the patient may still be at risk, getting help through the organisation's urgent process comes first. The report comes after.

## Steps

1. **Build the timeline.** List events in order, with times, as facts: what was seen, said and done. Keep what is known apart from what is believed or was heard second-hand.
2. **Use roles, not names**: "the patient", "Nurse A", "the doctor on call". Put a patient identifier only in the field the form requires.
3. **Record the immediate actions**, and who was told, such as the person in charge, the patient or their family.
4. **Describe the outcome as reported.** Use the organisation's harm categories only if they're provided, and mark the level "to confirm" for the reviewer.
5. **List possible contributing factors** across the system: the task, equipment, environment, staffing, communication, training and procedures. These are prompts for the review, not findings.
6. **Note what went well**, such as a quick escalation. That is learning too.
7. **Suggest questions for the review** to look at, never conclusions or actions against individuals.

## Output

```
# Patient-safety incident report (draft)
Date and time: <…>   Location: <…>   Reported by: <role>

## What happened
| Time | Event | Source (seen, told or record) |
|---|---|---|
## Immediate actions
## Outcome for the patient, as reported
## Possible contributing factors, for review
## What went well
## Who has been told, including the patient or family
## To confirm
```

## Rules

- **Facts, not blame.** Leave out judgement words such as "careless", "failed to" or "negligent". Write what happened, for example "The dose given was 10 mg; the prescription said 1 mg."
- Never invent times, doses, names, outcomes or causes. Mark gaps "to confirm".
- Never diagnose, or judge whether care was clinically right. That is for qualified reviewers, who check the report before it is final.
- Keep personal data to the minimum, and follow the organisation's incident policy and the health-data laws that apply.
- Being open with the patient and family follows the organisation's policy. Record whether it has happened; don't decide it.
- Decisions about individual staff belong to the organisation's own processes, not to this report.
