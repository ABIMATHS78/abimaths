# Patient information

Turn a clinician's material into a leaflet that patients can read, understand and act on. The clinician supplies the facts and approves the final version.

## Ask first

If any of this is missing, ask for it in one short message:
- the clinical material: notes, an existing leaflet, a procedure description or guidance the clinician has chosen;
- who the leaflet is for (age, language, reading or access needs) and when they'll receive it;
- the clinician or team who will approve it;
- the service name and contact details, or "to confirm".

If there's no clinical material, ask for it. Don't write the medical content from general knowledge.

## Steps

1. **List the questions patients ask**: what is it, why me, what happens before, during and after, what are the benefits, risks and alternatives, when should I get help, and who do I contact?
2. **Answer each question only from the material.** Where it gives no answer, write "to confirm" and add the question to a list for the clinician.
3. **Write plainly**: short sentences, everyday words, "you" and "we". Explain each medical term the first time it appears, or replace it.
4. **Present numbers with care.** Keep the clinician's figures exactly. A percentage can also be shown as a number of people, such as 2% as "about 2 in every 100 people", without changing its value.
5. **Lay it out for scanning**: question headings, short paragraphs, bullet points for steps, and the most important actions near the top.
6. **Make "when to get help" stand out**, using exactly the signs and contacts the clinician gives, plus the emergency number for the area where the leaflet will be used.
7. **Read it as an anxious patient in a hurry would**, and cut anything that doesn't help them.

## Output

```
# <Title in the patient's words>
This leaflet explains <…>. It doesn't replace advice from your care team.

## <One heading for each question from step 1>
## When to get help
## Contact us
<service, phone number, opening hours>

Approved by: <name and role, to confirm>
Date: <to confirm>   Review date: <to confirm>
```

After the leaflet, list every "to confirm" item and any place where the material was unclear.

## Rules

- Never diagnose, and never add treatment advice, risks, benefits or figures that aren't in the clinician's material.
- The leaflet stays a draft until a qualified clinician has checked and approved it.
- Use no patient details. A leaflet is for everyone it applies to, not for one person.
- Follow the organisation's style, accessibility and translation standards, and the health-information rules that apply where it will be used.
- Don't promise outcomes. Say "most people" or "some people", or give the figure provided.
