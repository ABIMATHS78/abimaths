# Health study summary

Summarise what a health study did, what it found and how much weight it can bear, so a clinician or other qualified reader can judge it quickly. Report; don't recommend.

## Ask first

If any of this is missing, ask for it in one short message:
- the study itself, ideally the full text rather than the abstract or a news story about it;
- who the summary is for, such as clinicians, researchers or a policy team;
- the length they want (suggest one page).

If only the abstract is available, say so at the top of the summary, because abstracts leave out most limitations.

## Steps

1. **Identify the design**: randomised trial, cohort, case-control, cross-sectional, systematic review, qualitative study, lab or animal study, or another. Say whether it was peer reviewed or is a preprint.
2. **Describe the population**: who took part, how many, where, how they were chosen, and who was left out.
3. **State the question and the comparison**: what was compared with what, and which outcomes were measured.
4. **Report the main results with the paper's own numbers**, including confidence intervals where given. Report absolute figures as well as relative ones when the paper gives both.
5. **List the limitations**: those the authors name, and others you can see, such as small numbers, short follow-up, people dropping out, confounding, or a narrow group of participants.
6. **Note the funding** and any declared conflicts of interest.
7. **Rate the strength of evidence** as stronger, moderate, limited or very limited, and give the reason. A single observational study shows a link, not cause and effect.
8. **Say what the study doesn't show**: the conclusions it can't support.

## Output

```
# <Study title>, <journal or preprint server>, <year>
Design: <…>   Peer reviewed: <yes, no or to confirm>
Question: <…>
Population: <…>
Main results: <numbers as reported>
Limitations: <…>
Funding and conflicts: <…>
Strength of evidence: <rating>, because <reason>
What it doesn't show: <…>
```

## Rules

- **No advice.** Never tell anyone to start, stop or change a treatment, test or habit. Conclusions for practice belong to qualified professionals.
- The summary is for a qualified professional to check against the paper before anyone relies on it.
- Never invent numbers, or round them into new ones. Copy them exactly, and mark anything you can't find "to confirm".
- Don't overstate. Write "was linked to" for associations, and "caused" only when the design supports it.
- Point out where the paper's conclusions go further than its results.
- If the paper includes details about individual patients, don't repeat anything that could identify them, and follow the health-data rules that apply.
