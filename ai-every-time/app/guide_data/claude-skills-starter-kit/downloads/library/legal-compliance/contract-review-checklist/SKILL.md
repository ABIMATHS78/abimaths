# Contract review against your checklist

Compare a contract with the organisation's own playbook, so the lawyer can spend their time on what matters. This is a first pass, not legal advice: a qualified lawyer reviews anything that matters before anyone signs.

## Ask first

If any of this is missing, ask for it in one short message:
- the contract, in its latest version;
- the organisation's clause checklist or playbook: preferred positions, acceptable fallbacks and red lines;
- the parties, what the deal is, its value, and any signing deadline.

If there's no checklist, offer a list of clauses commonly reviewed, clearly marked as generic, for the organisation's lawyer to turn into its own positions.

## Steps

1. **List every clause** with its number and heading, so nothing is skipped.
2. **Match each clause to the checklist**: matches, differs, missing from the contract, or not covered by the checklist.
3. **For each difference**, quote the contract's wording exactly, then give the checklist's preferred position and its fallback.
4. **Flag terms that often need a lawyer**: liability caps and exclusions, indemnities, automatic renewal, termination rights, intellectual property, exclusivity, data protection, payment terms, governing law and jurisdiction.
5. **Check the mechanics**: defined terms used consistently, cross-references that point to the right clause, schedules that are attached and named.
6. **List the questions** for the lawyer and for the other side.

## Output

```
## Summary
Red lines hit: <list, or "none found">
Top three issues: <list>

| Clause | The contract says (quoted) | Our position | Status | Note |
|---|---|---|---|---|

## Questions for the lawyer
## Questions for the other side
## To confirm
```

## Rules

- **Not legal advice.** Never say a contract is safe to sign; say what a lawyer should look at and why.
- Quote the contract exactly. Never present a summary as if it were the wording.
- Don't invent the organisation's positions. If the checklist doesn't cover a clause, say so.
- Keep the contract confidential, and include only what the review needs.
- Mark anything unclear "to confirm" rather than guessing what it means.
