# Privacy notice check

Find the gaps in a privacy notice before it goes live. This is a structured check, not legal advice: what the law requires depends on where the organisation and its users are, so the user confirms the checklist, and a lawyer or data protection officer has the final say.

## Ask first

If any of this is missing, ask for it in one short message:
- the privacy notice;
- what the organisation actually does with personal data: what it collects, why, from whom, who it shares it with, where it's stored and for how long;
- the places it operates in and serves, and the law or laws the user believes apply.

## Build the checklist

Offer points that data-protection laws commonly require, and ask the user to confirm, add or remove points for their jurisdiction:
- who the organisation is, and how to contact it and any data protection officer;
- what personal data is collected, and where it comes from;
- the purposes, and the legal basis for each where the law asks for one;
- who receives the data, and any transfers to other countries;
- how long the data is kept, or how that period is decided;
- people's rights, how to use them, and how to complain to the regulator;
- automated decisions or profiling, if any;
- children's data, cookies and tracking, if relevant;
- how changes to the notice are announced.

## Steps

1. **Map each confirmed point** to the place in the notice that covers it.
2. **Compare the notice with what actually happens.** A notice that omits a real use of data is a bigger gap than awkward wording.
3. **Check it's readable**: plain words, clear headings, easy to find.
4. **Draft wording for each gap**, marked as a draft for review.

## Output

```
| Point | Covered? | Where in the notice | Gap | Draft wording for review |
|---|---|---|---|---|

Differences between the notice and actual practice: <list>
To confirm with a lawyer or data protection officer: <list>
```

## Rules

- **Not legal advice.** Never state that a notice is compliant; report what the check found.
- Don't invent the organisation's processing. If you don't know whether something happens, ask, or mark it "to confirm".
- Don't name a single law as the only one that applies; the user confirms which apply.
