# Regulatory update summary

Turn a new rule or guidance document into a short brief a busy team can act on. It summarises; it doesn't give legal advice, and the organisation's lawyer or compliance lead decides what it means for them.

## Ask first

If any of this is missing, ask for it in one short message:
- the document itself, ideally from the official source, or its link;
- the organisation's sector, the places it operates and its main activities;
- any questions the team especially cares about.

## Steps

1. **Identify the document**: who issued it, its title, its date, and what it is (a law, a regulation, binding rules, guidance, a consultation or a draft). This decides how much weight it carries.
2. **Find the dates**: publication, when it comes into force, transition periods, and consultation deadlines.
3. **Set out the scope**: who it applies to, and any exemptions or thresholds.
4. **List what changes**, compared with what applied before where the document says so.
5. **List the obligations**, each with its section number, quoted or closely summarised, and its deadline.
6. **Note penalties or enforcement** only if the document states them.
7. **Consider the impact** on the organisation as questions and possible effects, not conclusions.
8. **Suggest actions to consider**, with suggested owners.

## Output

```
## <Title>, <issuer>, <date>
Type: <law / regulation / guidance / consultation / draft>
In force: <date, or "to confirm">

**In short**: <three sentences>

## Who it affects
## What changes
## Obligations and deadlines
| Section | Obligation | Applies to | Deadline |
|---|---|---|---|

## Questions for us
## Actions to consider
## Sources
```

## Rules

- **Not legal advice.** Flag where expert advice is needed.
- Separate binding rules from guidance, and final texts from drafts and consultations.
- Never guess a date or a threshold. Check the official source, or mark it "to confirm".
- Quote obligations accurately, with section numbers, so they can be checked.
