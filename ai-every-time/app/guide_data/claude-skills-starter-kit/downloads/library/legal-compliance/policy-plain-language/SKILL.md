# Policy in plain language

Make a policy easy to understand while keeping exactly what it requires. The plain version is for understanding; the policy's owner, and a lawyer where the policy has legal effect, approve it before use. This skill doesn't give legal advice.

## Ask first

If any of this is missing, ask for it in one short message:
- the policy, in full;
- who will read the plain version, and what they need to do with it;
- whether it will replace the original or sit beside it;
- any wording that must stay as it is, such as wording a regulator requires.

## Steps

1. **Find every obligation**: who must do what, by when, and under which conditions. Note the exceptions.
2. **Rewrite section by section**, keeping the original order so the two can be compared:
   - short sentences and the active voice;
   - "you" for the reader and "we" for the organisation, if that suits the audience;
   - each term explained the first time it appears;
   - numbers, amounts, deadlines and contact details copied exactly.
3. **Keep every condition and exception.** "Unless", "only if" and "except" change meaning; never drop them.
4. **Flag ambiguity.** Where the original could mean two things, don't pick one: list it for the owner.
5. **Check the result** against the obligation list from step 1. Each obligation must appear in the plain version, unchanged in effect.

## Output

```
## <Policy name>, in plain language
<The rewrite, with the original section numbers>

## What changed in the wording
| Section | Original | Plain version |
|---|---|---|

## For the policy owner
- Ambiguous points: <list>
- Wording kept as required: <list>
- To confirm: <list>
```

## Rules

- **Change the words, never the meaning**: not the scope, the deadlines, the rights or the duties.
- Not legal advice. Say so when the policy has legal effect, and leave approval to the owner and their lawyer.
- Don't add new rules, examples that imply new rules, or promises the original doesn't make.
- Keep the plain version clearly linked to the official policy, so readers know which one governs.
