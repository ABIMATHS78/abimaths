# Compliance evidence tracker

Turn a list of requirements and a pile of documents into one tracker that shows what's covered, what's missing and who's doing what. The tracker records evidence; whether the organisation is compliant is for its compliance owner, auditor or lawyer to decide. This skill doesn't give legal advice.

## Ask first

If any of this is missing, ask for it in one short message:
- the requirements: a standard, a regulation, a contract's obligations or an internal policy, with their reference numbers;
- the scope: which teams, systems or sites;
- the evidence collected so far: document names, dates, owners and where each is stored;
- the audit or review date.

## Steps

1. **List every requirement** with its reference, in the framework's order.
2. **Map the evidence** to each requirement: document name, date, owner and storage location. Point to where sensitive evidence lives instead of copying it.
3. **Set a status**:
   - **In place**: current evidence fully covers it;
   - **Partly**: some evidence, with a gap described;
   - **Missing**: no evidence yet;
   - **Out of date**: the evidence is older than the review cycle allows.
4. **Plan each gap**: the action, the owner and a due date before the audit.
5. **Summarise**: counts by status, the riskiest gaps first, and what's due this week.

## Output

```
| Ref | Requirement | Evidence (name, date, location) | Owner | Status | Gap and action | Due |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|

Summary: <n> in place · <n> partly · <n> missing · <n> out of date
Priority gaps: <list>
To confirm: <list>
```

## Rules

- **Never mark a requirement "in place" without evidence** you can point to.
- Never invent documents, dates or owners. Unknowns are "to confirm".
- Keep personal and confidential data out of the tracker itself.
- Not legal advice: the tracker says what evidence exists, not whether the law or standard is met.
- Keep the requirement wording exact, quoted from the source.
