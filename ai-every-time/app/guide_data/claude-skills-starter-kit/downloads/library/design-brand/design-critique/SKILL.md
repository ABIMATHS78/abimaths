# Design critique

Give feedback that improves the work: tied to its goals, specific about what to change, and respectful of the designer.

## Before you start

You need the design and its goal: who it's for, and what they should notice, feel or do. Ask about the brief, the stage of the work (an early idea or final polish), and any limits, such as brand rules or a fixed size. Ask in one short message.

## Steps

1. **Restate the goal** in one sentence, so the feedback is judged against it, not against taste.
2. **Start with what works**, specifically, so it's kept in the next version.
3. **Check the first glance**: what the eye goes to first, second and third. Does that order match what matters most?
4. **Review each area:**
   - hierarchy: size, weight, colour and space show what matters;
   - layout: alignment, grouping, spacing and balance;
   - type: legible sizes, few typefaces, comfortable line lengths;
   - colour: used with purpose, consistently, and with enough contrast;
   - copy: clear, short and true to the message;
   - accessibility: contrast, text size, and no information carried by colour alone.
5. **Make each point useful**: what you see, why it matters for the goal, and a suggestion. Ask a question where the designer may have had a reason.
6. **Rank the feedback**: the few changes that matter most first, then smaller polish.
7. **Match the stage.** For early work, focus on direction and structure, not pixel details.

## Output

```
Goal: <one sentence>

What works:
- <a specific strength>

Most important changes:
1. <what you see> → <why it matters> → <suggestion>

Polish:
- <smaller points>

Questions for the designer:
- <question>
```

## Rules

- Critique the work, never the person. Be honest and kind.
- Tie every point to the goal, the brief or a clear design principle. Label personal taste as taste.
- Be specific: "the price is smaller than the headline, so it's easy to miss", not "make it pop".
- Keep the list short enough to act on. Many points of equal weight help nobody.

## Checklist before you finish

- [ ] The feedback is judged against the stated goal.
- [ ] Strengths are named specifically.
- [ ] The most important changes come first.
- [ ] Every point has a reason and a suggestion.
