# Design brief

Give a designer everything they need to do great work the first time, and agree what "done" means before the work starts.

## Before you start

Ask for: what's being made and why, who it's for, where it will appear, the deadline and the budget, the brand guidelines, examples the team likes and dislikes, and who approves the work. Ask in one short message, and mark what's still unknown "to confirm".

## Steps

1. **State the problem**, not the solution: what's wrong now, or what's needed, and why it matters.
2. **Describe the audience**: who they are, what they care about, and what they think now.
3. **Write the single message**: the one thing the audience should take away, with up to three supporting points.
4. **Define the deliverables**: every item, with its format, size, file type and languages, and where it will appear, such as a 1080 × 1350 pixel social post or an A4 printed leaflet.
5. **List the requirements**: brand rules, required copy, logos, legal text, accessibility needs, and anything that mustn't appear.
6. **Give direction without designing it**: the feeling to aim for, with references the team likes and why. Leave room for the designer's own ideas.
7. **Set the process**: the rounds of feedback, who gives it, how decisions are made, and the dates.
8. **Say how success is judged**: what the work must achieve, and how that will be measured.

## Output

```
# Design brief: <project>
Owner: <name> · Approver: <name> · Due: <date> · Budget: <amount>

## The problem
## Audience
## Message
## Deliverables
| Item | Format | Size | Where it appears | Due |
|---|---|---|---|---|
## Requirements
## Direction and references
## Process and dates
## How we'll judge success
## To confirm
```

## Rules

- Never invent budgets, dates, sizes or approvals; mark gaps "to confirm".
- Use references for direction only. Don't ask a designer to copy someone else's work.
- Make sure the images, fonts and music used have the right licences.
- Keep the brief short enough to read in five minutes.

## Checklist before you finish

- [ ] The problem is stated, not just a solution.
- [ ] There's one main message.
- [ ] Every deliverable has a format, a size and a date.
- [ ] Success measures and approvers are named.
