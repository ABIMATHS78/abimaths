# SEO brief

Give a writer what they need to make the most useful page for one search, so it deserves to rank and moves readers to act.

## 1. Get what you need

If anything below is missing, ask for it in one short message. Mark smaller gaps "to confirm" and carry on.
- the topic or main keyword, and whether this is a new page or an update;
- what the business wants readers to do next, and who those readers are;
- keyword data from the person's own tools, such as search volume, if they have it;
- a list or sitemap of the site's pages, for internal links.

## 2. Find the search intent

Work out what someone typing the keyword wants: to learn, to compare options, to buy or act, or to reach a particular site. If you can search the web, study the pages that rank now: their type (guide, list, product page, tool), depth and angle. If you can't, say the intent is your judgement, to be checked against live results.

The page must match that intent. If the business goal doesn't fit it, say so: a sales page rarely ranks for a "how to" search.

## 3. Choose the keywords

- Pick one primary keyword, plus close variants and related terms the same page can cover naturally.
- Check no other page on the site already aims at the same keyword. If one does, suggest updating it instead.
- Quote search volumes only from the person's data. Otherwise write "volume: to confirm".

## 4. Plan the page

- Outline the headings and what each section must cover. Answer the main question early.
- List the questions to answer, from related searches, forums, and what customers ask sales and support staff.
- Add what the ranking pages lack: first-hand experience, original data, clearer steps, better examples.
- Suggest three to six internal links to and from the page, with natural anchor text. Use only pages that exist.
- Write two or three titles and meta descriptions. Put the primary keyword near the start, promise what the page delivers, and keep each short enough to show in full in results. Check current guidance on length rather than trusting a fixed number.

## 5. Return the brief

```
# SEO brief: <working title>
Primary keyword: <keyword> (volume: <figure or "to confirm">) | Related: <terms>
Search intent: <intent>, because <evidence from the results>
Reader and next step: <who>, <action> | Format: <format>, about <range> words
URL: /<slug>
Titles: 1. <title> 2. <title>
Meta descriptions: 1. <text> 2. <text>
Outline:
  H1: <heading>
  H2: <heading>: <what to cover>
Questions to answer: <list>
Internal links: <from page> to <to page>, anchor "<text>"
Sources and expertise: <sources to cite, who writes or reviews, first-hand material>
```

## Rules

- Never invent search volumes, rankings, statistics, sources or URLs. Mark gaps "to confirm".
- Write for people first: no keyword stuffing, hidden text or pages made only for search engines.
- Don't copy competitors' text or structure. Aim to be more useful than they are.
- Pages on health, money, law or safety need reliable sources and review by a qualified person.
- Claims about a product must be true and provable under the advertising and consumer-protection rules where the readers are.
