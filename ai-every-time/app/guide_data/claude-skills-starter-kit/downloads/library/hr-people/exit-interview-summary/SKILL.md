# Exit interview summary

Turn exit interview notes into a picture of why people leave and what would help others stay, without identifying anyone who left.

## Ask first

If any of this is missing, ask for it in one short message:
- the interview notes or form answers, with names removed;
- the period covered, the number of interviews, and the total number of leavers if known;
- the questions asked;
- who the summary is for, and the organisation's minimum group size for reporting.

## Steps

1. **Check anonymity.** Remove names, leaving dates, job titles and any detail that points to one person. In a small team, even "a senior engineer in the Lagos office" can identify someone. Break results down by group only when the group is large enough.
2. **Pull out, for each interview**: the main reason for leaving, other reasons, what they valued, what might have kept them, and their suggestions.
3. **Group the reasons into themes**, such as pay, progression, management, workload, flexibility, culture, or personal reasons such as moving or study.
4. **Count.** Give the number of leavers mentioning each theme, out of the total interviewed, and how many named it as their main reason.
5. **Separate what the organisation can influence** from what it can't, such as a partner's move abroad.
6. **Note what leavers valued.** It shows what to protect.
7. **Suggest actions** linked to the themes, each with a possible owner and a way to measure progress, for leaders to decide on.
8. **State the limits**: small numbers, who wasn't interviewed, and that leavers may not say everything.

## Output

```
# Exit interview summary, <period>
Interviews: <n> of <total> leavers

| Theme | Leavers mentioning it | Named as main reason | Can we influence it? |
|---|---|---|---|

## What leavers valued
## Suggested actions
| Action | Theme | Owner (to confirm) | How we'll know |
|---|---|---|---|
## Limits
```

## Rules

- **Never identify a leaver**, directly or by combining details, and don't attribute quotes.
- Never invent reasons, counts or quotes. Mark anything uncertain "to confirm".
- If an interview raises harassment, discrimination, fraud or a safety issue, flag it separately to the HR lead to handle under policy. Keep the details out of the themed summary.
- Don't use the summary to judge an individual manager or colleague. Concerns about a person go through the organisation's own process, with a person deciding.
- Follow the privacy and employment laws that apply where people worked, and any promise of confidentiality made to leavers.
