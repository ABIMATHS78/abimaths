# One-to-one prep

Help someone walk into a one-to-one with a clear agenda, good questions and their notes ready, so the time goes on what matters.

## Ask first

If any of this is missing, ask for it in one short message:
- who the meeting is with, and whether the person preparing is the manager or the team member;
- how long it is and how often they meet;
- the notes and actions from last time;
- current priorities, wins and problems;
- anything they want to raise, including feedback in either direction.

## Steps

1. **Review last time.** List each action and whether it's done, moving or stuck.
2. **Put the team member's topics first.** If the manager is preparing, leave room for the other person's agenda before their own.
3. **Start with how they are.** One open check-in question, and only what they choose to share.
4. **Cover priorities and blockers**: what's going well, what's stuck, and what help would move it.
5. **Prepare any feedback** as situation, behaviour and impact: one specific example, and a question that opens the conversation.
6. **Leave time for growth** at least every few meetings: skills, interests, career goals or a stretch task.
7. **Write open questions**, such as "What's taking more time than it should?" or "What would make next week easier?"
8. **Plan to close with actions**: what, who and by when, agreed by both people.

## Output

```
# One-to-one with <name or role>, <date>, <length>

## Actions from last time
| Action | Owner | Status |
|---|---|---|

## Agenda
1. Check-in (<minutes>)
2. Their topics (<minutes>)
3. Priorities and blockers (<minutes>)
4. Feedback, both ways (<minutes>)
5. Growth (<minutes>)
6. Actions (<minutes>)

## Questions to ask
## Notes
## Actions agreed
| Action | Owner | Due |
|---|---|---|
```

## Rules

- Never invent what was said or agreed before. If last time's notes are missing, mark earlier actions "to confirm".
- Keep sensitive information, such as health, family or personal problems, out of the notes. Record it only if the person agrees, and both the organisation's record-keeping policy and the privacy laws that apply allow it.
- A one-to-one doesn't decide pay, promotion or discipline. Those follow the organisation's process, with a person deciding.
- If something serious comes up, such as harassment, a safety concern or someone in crisis, follow the organisation's procedure and point to the right support.
- Keep the agenda short enough to finish in the time.
