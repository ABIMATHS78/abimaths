# Onboarding plan

Plan a new starter's first three months so they feel welcome, learn things in a sensible order, and know what good looks like at each stage.

## Ask first

If any of this is missing, ask for it in one short message:
- the role, team and start date, and whether they'll work on site, remotely or both;
- what success looks like at 90 days;
- the key people they should meet, by role;
- the systems, tools and required training, such as health and safety or data protection;
- any probation period or review points the organisation already uses.

If something is still unknown, make a sensible assumption, say what it is, and carry on.

## Steps

1. **Before day one**: equipment, accounts and access ready; a welcome message with the first day's time, place and contact; a buddy chosen.
2. **First week**: a welcome, introductions, the essentials of the role and the organisation, required training, and one small task they can finish.
3. **Days 1 to 30, learn**: meet the key people, and learn the work, the tools and how decisions are made. Goals are mostly about understanding, plus one or two early contributions.
4. **Days 31 to 60, contribute**: take on real pieces of work with support. Each goal names the piece of work and a date.
5. **Days 61 to 90, own**: run their core responsibilities with less support. Goals match what success at 90 days looks like.
6. **Set the check-ins**: a short weekly one-to-one, and a longer review at 30, 60 and 90 days that asks how it's going on both sides.
7. **Make every goal specific**: what, by when, and how both people will know it's done.
8. **Give every item an owner**: the manager, the buddy, HR, IT or the new starter.

## Output

```
# Onboarding plan for <role>, starting <date>
Manager: <role>   Buddy: <to confirm>

## Before day one
| Task | Owner | Done |
|---|---|---|

## Days 1–30: learn
| Goal | How we'll know | Owner | By |
|---|---|---|---|
People to meet: <roles>

## Days 31–60: contribute
## Days 61–90: own
## Check-ins: <weekly one-to-one; reviews at 30, 60 and 90 days>
```

## Rules

- Never invent policies, benefits, training courses or legal requirements. Mark them "to confirm with HR".
- Keep sensitive personal information out of the plan. Ask privately, through the proper channel, whether the new starter needs any adjustments, and don't record the reasons in the plan.
- Pre-employment checks and required training vary by country and sector. Follow the organisation's HR process and the employment laws that apply.
- Make no assumptions about background, age, family or experience beyond what the role requires.
- Decisions about probation, pay or continued employment stay with the manager and HR.
- Keep it realistic. Fewer, clearer goals are easier to follow.
