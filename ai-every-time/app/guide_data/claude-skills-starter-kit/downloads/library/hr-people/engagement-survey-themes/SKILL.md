# Engagement survey themes

Turn a pile of survey comments into a short, honest picture of what people are saying, without anyone being identifiable.

## Ask first

If any of this is missing, ask for it in one short message:
- the comments, exported without names, email addresses or employee IDs;
- the survey questions the comments answer;
- how many people were invited and how many responded, if known;
- the organisation's minimum group size for reporting results;
- any breakdowns needed, such as by department.

## Steps

1. **Protect anonymity first.** Before analysing, remove or blur names, specific events, job titles and any detail that could point to one person. If a breakdown would show a group smaller than the minimum size, merge it with another or leave it out. If there's no rule, don't report any group under five.
2. **Read every comment** before choosing themes.
3. **Code each comment** to one or more themes, such as workload, management, pay and recognition, growth, communication, tools or culture. Name themes in the words people actually use.
4. **Count accurately.** For each theme, give the number of comments and its share of all comments, and note that one comment can count towards more than one theme.
5. **Note the tone** of each theme: mostly positive, mostly negative or mixed.
6. **Choose short quotes** that represent each theme fairly, lightly edited to remove anything identifying. Don't pick only the most extreme.
7. **Separate what people said from what it might mean.** Put interpretation in its own section.
8. **Suggest actions and questions to explore**, for leaders to decide on.

## Output

```
# Survey themes for <survey>, <period>
Responses: <n> of <invited>   Comments: <n>

| Theme | Comments | Share | Tone | What people said |
|---|---|---|---|---|

## Quotes (anonymised)
## What this might mean
## Suggested actions and questions to explore
## Limits: <response rate, small groups, who may not have answered>
```

## Rules

- **Never try to identify anyone**, and decline any request to work out who wrote a comment.
- Never invent comments, quotes or numbers. Mark gaps "to confirm".
- Don't overstate. Say "most" only for more than half; otherwise give the number.
- If a comment describes harassment, discrimination or a safety risk, tell the HR lead it exists, so it can be handled under policy. Don't repeat identifying details in the report.
- Follow the privacy laws that apply where people work, and the confidentiality promised in the survey.
- Leaders decide the actions. Survey comments are never used to judge an individual.
