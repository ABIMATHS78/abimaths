# Interview questions

Interview every candidate fairly and consistently, so decisions rest on evidence about the job, not on gut feeling.

## Before you start

You need the job description, and the four to six skills or qualities that matter most. Ask about the interview stages, who interviews at each one, the time available, and any work sample or task that's planned. Ask in one short message.

## Steps

1. **Choose what to assess** at this stage: four to six skills or qualities, each defined in a sentence.
2. **Write one or two core questions for each**, asked of every candidate in the same way:
   - questions about past experience, such as "Tell me about a time you…";
   - questions about a situation, such as "What would you do if…";
   - for technical skills, a short practical task related to the real work.
3. **Add follow-up prompts** that draw out specific evidence: what did you do, why, what happened, and what would you change?
4. **Describe what good looks like** for each question: signs of a strong, an acceptable and a weak answer, so every interviewer scores against the same standard.
5. **Plan the time**: introductions, the questions, the candidate's own questions, and next steps.
6. **Add a scoring sheet**: each skill rated on a defined scale, with notes on the evidence, completed by each interviewer alone before they discuss.

## Output

```
# Interview plan: <role>, <stage>
Time: <minutes> · Interviewers: <names or roles>

| Skill | Question | Follow-ups | A strong answer shows |
|---|---|---|---|

Scoring: 1 = no evidence · 2 = some evidence · 3 = clear evidence · 4 = strong evidence
```

## Rules

- **Never ask about protected characteristics**: age, family plans or caring duties, marital status, pregnancy, religion, ethnicity, nationality beyond the legal right to work, sexual orientation, disability or health. Ask only whether the candidate can do the job's essential tasks, with or without adjustments.
- Ask every candidate the same core questions.
- Avoid trick questions and puzzles with no link to the job.
- Offer adjustments to the process for candidates who need them.
- A person makes every hiring decision. Interview rules vary by country, so check the ones that apply.

## Checklist before you finish

- [ ] Every question links to a skill the job needs.
- [ ] Each question has signs of a strong answer.
- [ ] Interviewers score alone before discussing.
- [ ] No question touches a protected characteristic.
