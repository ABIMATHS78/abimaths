# Offer letter draft

Put the agreed offer in writing, clearly and accurately, so the candidate knows exactly what they're accepting.

## Before you start

You need the agreed terms: the job title, the manager, the start date, the location and working pattern, the pay and how often it's paid, any bonus or commission, benefits, holiday, probation, the notice period, and conditions such as references or the right to work. Ask for the organisation's offer letter template, and the deadline to accept. Ask in one short message.

## Steps

1. **Use the organisation's template**, if there is one, and its contracts as the source of standard terms.
2. **Open warmly**, with the offer stated clearly in the first paragraph.
3. **Set out the terms** in the order the candidate will look for them: the role, start date, pay, working pattern and location, benefits, holiday, probation and notice.
4. **List the conditions** the offer depends on, and what the candidate needs to provide.
5. **Explain how to accept**, by when, and whom to contact with questions.
6. **List every figure and date** for HR to check against what was agreed.

## Output

```
<Date>
Dear <name>,

<the offer letter>

For HR to check:
- <each figure, date and condition, and where it came from>
- <terms that weren't provided, marked "to confirm">
```

## Rules

- **Use only the agreed terms.** Never invent pay, benefits, dates or conditions; mark gaps "to confirm".
- An offer letter can form part of an employment contract. A qualified HR or legal person must review it before it's sent, because employment law varies by country.
- Don't include promises that weren't agreed, such as future pay rises or promotions.
- Keep the candidate's personal details confidential.

## Checklist before you finish

- [ ] Every term matches what was agreed, or is marked "to confirm".
- [ ] The conditions and the deadline to accept are clear.
- [ ] The figures are listed for HR to check.
- [ ] The letter goes to HR or legal before it goes to the candidate.
