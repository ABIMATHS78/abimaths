# Job description

Write an advert that tells the right people what the job really is, so they apply, and lets people who wouldn't fit see that it isn't for them.

## Before you start

Ask for: the job title, the team and the manager; why the role exists, and what success looks like after six and twelve months; the main tasks; the skills truly needed on day one; the pay range, location, working pattern and benefits; and the hiring process, with dates. Ask in one short message.

## Steps

1. **Start with the purpose**: why the role exists, and the difference it makes.
2. **Describe the work**: five to eight responsibilities in plain words, the most important first.
3. **Separate must-haves from nice-to-haves.** Keep the must-haves to what's truly needed on day one. Long lists put off good candidates, especially those who only apply when they meet every point.
4. **Describe skills, not proxies.** Ask for what someone can do, rather than years of experience or a degree, unless the job really needs them, such as a licence the law requires.
5. **Be clear about the deal**: the pay range, location, remote or hybrid work, hours, benefits and chances to grow.
6. **Explain the process**: the stages, the dates, and how to ask for adjustments to the process.
7. **Check the language**: plain words, no jargon or internal names, and no words that suggest a particular age, gender or background, such as "young and energetic" or "digital native".

## Output

```
# <Job title>
<Team> · <Location and working pattern> · <Pay range>

## Why this role matters
## What you'll do
## What you'll bring
Must-have:
Nice to have:
## What we offer
## How to apply
<Stages, dates, and how to ask for adjustments>
```

## Rules

- Never invent pay, benefits or facts about the organisation; mark gaps "to confirm".
- Don't include requirements about age, gender, family status, religion, ethnicity, health or any other protected characteristic, unless the law allows a specific exception.
- Some places require pay ranges in adverts, or ban certain requirements. Ask the hiring team to check the employment laws that apply.
- Describe the job honestly, including the hard parts.

## Checklist before you finish

- [ ] The must-haves are only what's needed on day one.
- [ ] The pay, location and working pattern are clear, or marked "to confirm".
- [ ] The language is inclusive and free of jargon.
- [ ] The process and adjustments are explained.
