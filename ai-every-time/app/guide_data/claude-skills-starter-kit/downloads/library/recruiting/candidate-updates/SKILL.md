# Candidate updates

Treat every candidate with respect, including the ones who don't get the job, so they'd apply again and speak well of you.

## Before you start

You need the stage, the candidate's name, the role, and the facts to share: dates, times, the format and who they'll meet, or the decision. For a rejection, ask whether feedback can be shared, and what it is. Ask in one short message.

## Steps

1. **Pick the message**: application received, an invitation to interview, the next stage, a delay, a rejection, or an offer to follow.
2. **Lead with the news** in the first sentence, especially when it's a no.
3. **Give every practical detail**: the date, the time and time zone, the format, how long it takes, who they'll meet, what to prepare, and how to ask for adjustments.
4. **For a delay**, say when they'll hear next, and keep that promise.
5. **For a rejection**, thank them, make clear the decision is final for this role, and share brief, job-related feedback if the organisation allows it. Mention other roles only if you mean it.
6. **Keep it warm and short**, and sign it from a real person, with a way to reply.

## Output

```
Subject: <role> at <organisation>: <the news>

<message>
```

## Rules

- Never give a reason for a decision that relates to a protected characteristic, such as age, gender, disability, religion, ethnicity or family status. Keep feedback to the job's requirements.
- Don't promise what isn't certain: an offer, a date or a future role.
- Never invent feedback, dates or details; mark gaps "to confirm".
- Tell everyone who was interviewed the outcome, rather than leaving them waiting.
- Keep candidates' details confidential.

## Checklist before you finish

- [ ] The news comes first.
- [ ] Every practical detail is included.
- [ ] Any feedback is about the job only.
- [ ] The tone is warm, and the next step is clear.
