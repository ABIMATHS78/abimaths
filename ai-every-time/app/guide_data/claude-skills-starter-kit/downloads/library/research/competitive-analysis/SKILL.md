# Competitive analysis

Show how a product compares with the alternatives customers actually consider, using evidence anyone can check. The goal is a better decision, not a longer table.

## Before you start

Ask in one short message for anything missing: the product and its target customer, the competitors to include (or the market, so you can suggest some), the decision this supports, and the features buyers care about most. If you can't search the web, ask the person to share pricing pages, product pages or reviews.

## Steps

1. **Choose the competitors.** Include direct competitors, indirect alternatives, and the option of doing nothing new (a spreadsheet, an agency, doing it by hand). Four to eight is usually enough.
2. **Fix the comparison points.** Features buyers care about, pricing model and price points, target customer, main promise, and strengths and weaknesses. Use the same points for every competitor.
3. **Gather evidence.** Start with primary sources: each competitor's own website, pricing page, documentation and public filings. Use reviews and independent reports to see how customers view them. Record the source and the date checked for every fact.
4. **Fill the table.** Write "not found" where you couldn't confirm something. Note the conditions on prices: billing period, currency, per user or flat rate, tax, discounts.
5. **Describe the positioning.** For each competitor, one sentence on who they're for and why buyers choose them, using their own messaging where you can.
6. **Draw conclusions.** Where the product is stronger, where it's behind, needs nobody meets, and threats to watch. Link every conclusion to evidence in the table.

## Rules

- Never invent a feature, price, customer number, review or testimonial. If you can't confirm it, write "not found" or "to confirm".
- Prices and features change often. Give the date checked for each, and suggest checking again before a decision.
- Use only public or properly obtained information. Never use fake sign-ups, misleading enquiries or confidential knowledge from a former employee.
- Be fair. Describe competitors' strengths honestly, and keep claims about them accurate and checkable, as laws on comparative advertising and unfair competition may apply.
- Don't collect personal data about competitors' staff or customers.
- Keep facts apart from your interpretation, and say when a point is your judgement.

## Checklist before you finish

- [ ] Every cell has a source and date, or says "not found".
- [ ] Competitors are compared on the same points.
- [ ] Each conclusion links to evidence in the table.
