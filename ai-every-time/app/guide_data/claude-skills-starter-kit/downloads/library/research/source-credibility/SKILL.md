# Source credibility

Decide how much weight a source can bear, and explain why, so the person can judge for themselves. Rate the source for a specific claim and a specific use, not as a label for the whole publisher.

## Before you start

You need the source (a link, text or file) and the claim the person wants to rely on. If it isn't clear what they'll use it for, ask in one short message: a social media post, a business decision and an academic paper need different standards.

## Steps

1. **Identify it.** Who wrote it, who published it, when, and what kind of source it is: research, official statistics, news, opinion, marketing or a forum post.
2. **Check the author.** Look for relevant expertise, a track record, and whether they're named at all. Check what independent sources say about them, not just their own biography.
3. **Check the evidence.** Does it give data, methods and sources you can trace? Do claims rest on primary evidence, or on other people's summaries? Do the numbers support the conclusion?
4. **Check for bias and interests.** Who paid for it, who gains if people believe it, and whether it treats opposing evidence fairly. Loaded language and missing counter-arguments are warning signs.
5. **Check the date.** When was it published or last updated? Is that recent enough for this topic, or has newer evidence replaced it?
6. **Corroborate.** Look for independent sources that confirm or contradict the key claim. Ten outlets repeating one original source count as one source.
7. **Rate it.** Give a rating and the main reasons, and say what would change your view.

## Rules

- Never invent an author's credentials, a funder, a date or a corroborating source. If you can't check something, write "not found" or "to confirm", and lower your confidence.
- If you can't search the web, say so, and base the rating only on the material provided.
- Don't judge by looks. A polished website or a confident tone isn't evidence.
- Apply the same tests to sources you agree with and sources you don't.
- Choose "Can't tell" when the evidence is too thin, rather than forcing a rating.
- Be careful with claims about real people. Stick to what the sources show, leave private lives alone, and remember that privacy and defamation laws may apply.

## Checklist before you finish

- [ ] Each check has a finding, or says what couldn't be checked.
- [ ] Corroborating sources are independent of each other.
- [ ] The rating fits the person's intended use.
