# Research plan

Write a plan that shows what you want to learn, how you'll learn it, and why the approach is sound and ethical, so others can approve it, fund it or take part.

## Before you start

Ask in one short message for anything missing: the decision or problem behind the research, who the plan is for, the time and budget available, and any limits, such as access to participants or data. If something isn't known yet, carry on and mark it "to confirm".

## Steps

1. **State the purpose.** One paragraph on the background, what isn't known, and the decision the research will inform.
2. **Write the research questions.** Three to five questions the study can actually answer, from the main question down. If you have hypotheses, state them so they can be tested.
3. **Choose the methods.** Match each question to a method: interviews or observation for "why" and "how", surveys or usage data for "how many", experiments for cause and effect. Give the reason for each choice and its main limitation.
4. **Define the sample.** Who takes part, the criteria for including or excluding people, how many and why that number suits the method, and how you'll recruit them without skewing the sample.
5. **Plan the analysis.** For each question, say how the data will be analysed and what kind of result would change the decision.
6. **Set the timeline.** Phases in weeks, with an owner for each and the dependencies between them. Allow extra time for recruitment, which often takes longer than planned.
7. **Cover the ethics.** Consent, data handling and risks to participants, as set out in the rules below. Say whether a formal ethics review is needed, and who would carry it out.
8. **List the risks.** What could go wrong, how likely it is, and what you'll do about it.

## Output

```
# Research plan: <title>
**Owner**: <name or role>   **Status**: Draft

1. Purpose and background
2. Research questions (and hypotheses, if any)
3. Methods, and why each was chosen
4. Sample and recruitment
5. Analysis plan
6. Timeline (phase, activities, weeks, owner)
7. Ethics and data protection
8. Risks and how to reduce them
9. Budget (amounts, or "to confirm")
10. Deliverables (what will be shared, with whom, and when)
```

## Rules

- Get informed consent. Tell participants the purpose, what taking part involves, how their data will be used and stored, and that they can stop at any time without penalty.
- Collect only the data you need, keep it secure, decide how long to keep it, and anonymise or pseudonymise it where you can. Follow the data protection laws and research ethics rules that apply where the research takes place.
- Take extra care with children, vulnerable people and sensitive topics, and check whether formal ethics approval is required.
- Keep incentives fair and modest, so they don't pressure anyone to take part.
- Don't promise results. Describe what the research could find, not what you hope it will find.
- Never invent sample sizes, costs, dates or earlier findings. Mark unknowns "to confirm".

## Checklist before you finish

- [ ] Every research question has a method and an analysis plan.
- [ ] The sample size is justified for the method chosen.
- [ ] Consent, data handling and ethics review are covered.
