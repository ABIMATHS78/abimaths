# Variance explainer

Turn a budget-versus-actual report into a clear story: what's different, why, and whether it matters.

## Ask first

If any of this is missing, ask for it in one short message:
- the budget and the actual figures for the period, by line, and year to date if available;
- the threshold for explaining a variance, such as over 10% or over a set amount;
- notes from budget holders on known reasons;
- who will read the explanation.

## Steps

1. **Calculate each variance** as an amount and a percentage, and label it favourable or unfavourable. For costs, spending less than budget is favourable; for income, more is favourable.
2. **Pick the lines that cross the threshold.** Explain these; group the small ones as "other".
3. **Sort each variance by type**:
   - **timing**: the spend or income will happen, just in a different month;
   - **volume**: more or fewer units, customers or hours than planned;
   - **price or rate**: the cost or price per unit changed;
   - **one-off**: an unplanned event;
   - **error or misposting**: something booked to the wrong line or period.
4. **Give the reason** from the budget holders' notes or the data. Where no reason is known, list it as a question for the budget holder.
5. **Say what it means for the full year**: will it reverse, persist or grow?
6. **Suggest follow-ups**: corrections to book, forecasts to update, questions to ask.

## Output

```
## <Period>: budget versus actual
Overall: <one-sentence summary>

| Line | Budget | Actual | Variance | % | F/U | Type | Reason |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|

## Full-year view
## Questions for budget holders
## Corrections to book
```

## Rules

- **Use only the figures provided.** Never invent numbers or reasons; mark unknown reasons "to confirm".
- Keep amounts in their currency and show the period clearly.
- Don't present a timing difference as a saving.
- This explains figures; it's not tax, investment or audit advice.
