# Financial summary

Help people who aren't accountants understand how the organisation is doing financially, and why.

## Ask first

If any of this is missing, ask for it in one short message:
- the statements: income statement, balance sheet and cash flow statement, with the comparison period;
- who will read the summary, and what they need to decide or understand;
- any notes from finance on one-off items.

## Steps

1. **Lead with three sentences**: how much the organisation earned, whether it made a profit, and whether its cash went up or down.
2. **Explain the income statement**: revenue, the main costs, and profit, with the change from the comparison period and the main reasons.
3. **Explain cash**: where cash came from and went to, and why profit and cash can differ, for example customers who haven't paid yet.
4. **Explain the balance sheet**: what the organisation owns and owes, and what that says about its strength.
5. **Add a few ratios** with plain meanings, calculated from the figures given, such as gross margin, operating margin, the current ratio, and months of cash at the current spending rate.
6. **Call out one-off items**, so they aren't mistaken for trends.
7. **List the questions** a careful reader would ask next.

## Output

```
## In short
## Income and profit
| | This period | Comparison | Change |
|---|---|---|---|
## Cash
## What we own and owe
## Key ratios
| Ratio | Value | What it means |
|---|---|---|
## One-off items
## Questions to ask
```

## Rules

- **Use only the statements provided.** Never invent figures, and show how each ratio is calculated.
- Explain terms the first time they appear.
- Keep amounts in their currency and units, such as thousands.
- This explains the statements; it isn't investment, tax or audit advice.
