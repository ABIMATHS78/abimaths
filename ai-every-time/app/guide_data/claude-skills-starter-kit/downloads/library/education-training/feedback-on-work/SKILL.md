# Feedback on work

Draft feedback that shows a learner where their work stands and what to do next. The teacher reviews and edits it before the learner sees it.

## Ask first

If any of this is missing, ask for it in one short message:
- the learner's work, with their name removed;
- the rubric or marking criteria, and the task brief;
- the learner's level;
- the school's feedback style, such as "what went well" and "even better if", and how long it should be;
- anything the teacher wants the feedback to focus on.

## Steps

1. **Read the brief and the rubric first**, so you judge the work against what was asked.
2. **Read the whole piece once** before commenting.
3. **Find evidence for each criterion.** Quote a short phrase, or point to a paragraph, section or question number. If a criterion has no evidence, say so.
4. **Suggest a level for each criterion**, naming the descriptor it matches and why, and how confident you are. Where the work sits between two levels, say which two and why.
5. **Name two or three strengths specifically**: "Your second paragraph links the cause to its effect with evidence," not "Good work."
6. **Choose one to three next steps**: the changes that would move the work up most. Each says what to do and links to a criterion.
7. **Add one task the learner can do now** to act on the feedback, such as redrafting one paragraph or correcting two questions.
8. **Write for the learner**: short sentences, and no rubric jargon unless the class uses it.

## Output

```
## For the teacher (not to share)
| Criterion | Evidence | Suggested level | Confidence |
|---|---|---|---|
Points to check: <anything unclear, or that needs the teacher's judgement>

## Draft feedback for the learner
What went well: <two or three specific strengths>
Next steps: <one to three actions, each linked to a criterion>
Your task now: <one small task>
```

## Rules

- This is a draft. The teacher reviews it and decides every mark and grade.
- Never invent quotes or say the work does something it doesn't. Quote exactly, and mark anything you can't judge from the work "to confirm".
- Don't rewrite the learner's work. Show what to improve and how, and let them do it.
- If something suggests copying or undeclared AI use, don't accuse anyone. Writing style alone isn't evidence. Note the specific reason neutrally under "Points to check".
- Be kind, honest and specific. Comment on the work, never on the person.
- Remove names and personal details, and follow the school's rules and the data protection laws that apply on sharing learner work with an AI tool.
