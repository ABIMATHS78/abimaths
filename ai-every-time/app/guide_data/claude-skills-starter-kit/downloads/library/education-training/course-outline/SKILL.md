# Course outline

Outline a course in which every module builds towards clear outcomes, and every assessment tests what was taught.

## Ask first

If any of this is missing, ask for it in one short message:
- the subject, and who the course is for: their level and what they already know;
- the overall goal: what learners will be able to do at the end;
- the length (weeks, hours a week, total hours) and how it's delivered;
- any curriculum, standard or qualification it must meet;
- assessment rules, such as required types, weightings or pass marks.

If something is still unknown, make a sensible assumption, say what it is, and carry on.

## Steps

1. **Write the course outcomes.** Four to eight statements of what learners will be able to do, each with a verb that can be assessed, such as "design", "evaluate" or "calculate", rather than "understand".
2. **Sequence the modules.** Start with foundations and build towards application. Each module has a clear purpose and depends only on what came before it.
3. **Fill in each module**: two or three outcomes, the main content, learning activities, and its share of the total hours.
4. **Plan the assessments.** Mix low-stakes checks along the way, such as quizzes, drafts and practice tasks, with the assessments that count. Each assessed piece tests one or more course outcomes.
5. **Map outcomes to assessments.** Fix any outcome that is never assessed, and any assessment that tests something never taught.
6. **Check the workload.** Teaching, independent study and assessment time must fit the hours available.
7. **List resources by type**, such as a core textbook, practice datasets or case studies, and say which ones the course team must choose.
8. **Note what learners need before they start**, and how they can catch up if they're missing something.

## Output

```
# <Course title>
For: <audience and entry level>
Length and delivery: <weeks, hours, mode>
Course outcomes: <numbered list>

| Module | Outcomes | Main content | Activities | Hours |
|---|---|---|---|---|

| Assessment | Type | Weight | When | Outcomes tested |
|---|---|---|---|---|

Prerequisites: <…>
Resources: <types; titles to confirm>
Open questions: <anything to confirm>
```

## Rules

- Never invent book titles, authors, accreditation requirements, credit values or regulations. Mark them "to confirm".
- Every outcome is taught, practised and assessed, and nothing is assessed that wasn't taught.
- Keep the outline realistic for the hours given. If the goal needs more time, say so.
- Plan for accessibility from the start: varied formats, captions and transcripts, and assessments that don't disadvantage learners for reasons unrelated to the outcomes.
- If past learner data shapes the course, use it only in summary form, without names, and follow the data protection rules that apply.
- The course team and the institution's own approval process decide what is taught and how it is graded.
