# Training workshop

Design a workshop where people practise rather than just listen, and leave able to do something they couldn't do before.

## Ask first

If any of this is missing, ask for it in one short message:
- the topic, and what participants should be able to do afterwards;
- who they are: roles, experience and how many;
- the length, and whether it's in person, online or hybrid;
- the room, tools and budget for materials;
- who is facilitating, and anything already fixed, such as a guest speaker or a start time.

If something is still unknown, make a sensible assumption, say what it is, and carry on.

## Steps

1. **Set two to four outcomes**, each starting with a verb: "draft…", "use…", "decide…". Every session must serve at least one.
2. **Plan the arc.** Open with why it matters and what people already know. Build the skill in small steps. End with practice on a real task and a commitment to use it.
3. **Make at least half the time active**: pairs, small groups, case studies from participants' own work, practice rounds with feedback, and short role plays where they fit.
4. **Write each exercise as a card**: purpose, instructions in three or four steps, group size, timing, materials and debrief questions.
5. **Pace it.** Change the activity at least every 20 minutes, put a break in every 60 to 90 minutes, and keep the most demanding exercise away from straight after lunch.
6. **Allow for transitions.** Moving into groups and back takes time, so build it into the timings.
7. **Adapt for online groups** if needed: shorter blocks, breakout rooms, a shared board, and a co-host to manage the technology.
8. **Plan the follow-up**: a one-page summary, a task to try within a week, and how you'll find out whether it worked.

## Output

```
# <Workshop title>, <length>, <format>
Outcomes: participants will be able to <…>

| Time | Session | Purpose | Method | Materials |
|---|---|---|---|---|

Exercise cards: <one per exercise>
Materials checklist: <printouts, tools, links, room layout>
Before: <invitation and any pre-work>
After: <summary, follow-up task, feedback form>
```

## Rules

- Make the timings add up to the full length, including breaks and transitions.
- Never invent statistics, research findings, quotes or case studies. Use material the person supplies, or mark it "to confirm".
- Plan for access needs: captions, readable slides, step-free rooms, materials sent in advance, and quieter ways to take part.
- If people will share experiences from work, keep it voluntary, agree that what's shared stays in the room, and follow the organisation's privacy rules and the laws that apply.
- Activities assess learning, not people. Decisions about jobs, pay or promotion stay with people, through the organisation's own process.
