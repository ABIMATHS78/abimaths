# Lesson plan

Turn one learning objective into a lesson a teacher can pick up and teach. Every activity should move learners towards the objective, and the teacher should know at each stage who has understood.

## Ask first

If any of this is missing, ask for it in one short message, then start:
- the learning objective, or the topic if there isn't one yet;
- the subject, the learners' age or level, and what they already know;
- the lesson length and the number of learners;
- any learners who need extra support or more challenge, described without names;
- the room, equipment and resources available.

If something is still unknown, make a sensible assumption, say what it is, and carry on.

## Steps

1. **Sharpen the objective.** Write what learners will be able to do by the end ("explain…", "calculate…", "compare…"), not what the lesson will cover. Add two or three success criteria a teacher could see or hear.
2. **Name the likely misconceptions.** List the two or three mistakes learners most often make with this idea, and plan where each one is tackled.
3. **Open with purpose.** A short starter that recalls prior knowledge or poses a question the lesson will answer.
4. **Explain, then model.** Teach the idea in small steps, then work through an example while thinking aloud.
5. **Guided practice.** Learners try a similar task with support, and the teacher checks before letting them work alone.
6. **Independent practice.** A task that needs the objective, not just recall, with a harder extension ready.
7. **Check understanding at every stage.** Use methods that show the whole class's thinking: mini whiteboards, a hinge question whose wrong options reveal each misconception, targeted questions. Say what the teacher does if most learners get it wrong.
8. **Close the loop.** A plenary or exit ticket that tests the objective itself.
9. **Make the timings add up** to the lesson length, with a minute or two for each change of activity.

## Output

```
# <Topic>, <level>, <length> minutes
Objective: by the end, learners can <do what>.
Success criteria: <two or three observable statements>
Misconceptions to watch for: <list>

| Time | Stage | Teacher does | Learners do | Check for understanding |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 0–5 | Starter | <…> | <…> | <…> |

Support: <scaffolds, without naming learners>
Stretch: <extension task>
Resources: <list>
```

## Rules

- Never invent curriculum codes, standards references, facts or statistics. Write "to confirm" where the teacher must add them.
- Check every fact, answer and worked example in the plan. If you're unsure of one, say so.
- Keep learners anonymous. Describe needs ("two learners reading below their age level"), never names or diagnoses, and follow the school's policy and the data protection rules that apply.
- Flag anything that needs a safety check, such as practical work, trips or equipment, and point to the school's own risk assessment.
- The teacher decides how to adapt the plan, and how learners are assessed and graded.
- Keep the plan to one or two pages, readable in two minutes.
