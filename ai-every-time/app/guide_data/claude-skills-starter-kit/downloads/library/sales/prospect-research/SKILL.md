# Prospect research

Build a short, accurate picture of a company and a person, so outreach or a call is relevant and every claim can be traced to a source.

## 1. Get what you need

If anything below is missing, ask for it in one short message. Mark smaller gaps "to confirm" and carry on.
- the company, and the contact's name and role;
- what the seller offers, and to whom;
- what the research is for: a first email, a call or a meeting;
- what's already known, such as past contact or notes in the CRM.

## 2. Research the company

If you can browse, start with primary sources: the company's own site, annual reports and filings, official company registers, press releases, job adverts and reputable news. If you can't, say so, work from what you're given, and list what to check.

Find out what they do and for whom, their size, locations and ownership, recent news (funding, launches, leadership changes, expansion or cuts) and the priorities they state in public. Job adverts often show which tools they use and what they're building.

## 3. Find reasons to talk now

Look for trigger events: a new leader, a hiring push, a new market, a new rule affecting their industry, a goal they've announced. Note the date and source of each.

## 4. Research the contact

Find their role, remit, time in the job and previous roles, and what they've said in public about their priorities in talks, articles, interviews or professional posts. Professional information only.

## 5. Judge the fit

Link what the seller offers to the company's likely priorities. Write these as hypotheses with a confidence level, not as facts. List anything that rules the company out, such as being too small, in the wrong region or tied to a competitor.

## 6. Return the research

```
# Prospect research: <company>, <contact>
Researched: <date>
Snapshot: <what they do, size, location, ownership, in two or three lines>
## Why now
- <trigger>, <date>. Source: <link or document>
## The contact
- <role and remit>. Source: <link>
- Public priorities: <what they said, where, when>
## Fit hypotheses
- <likely problem>: <how the seller might help>. Confidence: <high, medium or low>, because <reason>
## Red flags
- <item>
## Angle and opening question
<angle>. <question>
## To confirm
- <gap>
```

## Rules

- Never invent facts, figures, quotes or news. Every fact gets a source and a date, and hypotheses are labelled as such.
- Keep it professional. Leave out home addresses, family, health, religion, politics and personal social media, even when they're public.
- Follow the data-protection and privacy laws where the contact lives and where the seller works. Collect only what's needed, and don't guess or buy email addresses where that's unlawful.
- Respect websites' terms of use. Don't scrape where they forbid it.
- Note how old each fact is, and flag anything that may be out of date.
