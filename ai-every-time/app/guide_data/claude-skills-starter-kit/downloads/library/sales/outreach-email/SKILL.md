# Outreach email

Write a first email a busy person is glad they read, and follow-ups that add something each time.

## 1. Get what you need

If anything below is missing, ask for it in one short message. Mark smaller gaps "to confirm" and carry on.
- who it's for: name, role, company, and any research;
- what the seller offers, in plain words, and the problem it solves;
- a genuine reason to write now, and one proof point the seller can share;
- the ask: a reply, a short call, or an introduction to the right person;
- the sender's name, role and usual tone, and any past contact.

## 2. Find the reason

Write one sentence on why this person, and why now, based on something specific and true. If there's no real reason, say so and suggest what research might find one. A generic email to the wrong person costs the sender's reputation.

## 3. Write the first email

- **Subject**: short, plain and specific to them. No tricks.
- **Opening**: about them, not the sender. Skip "I hope this finds you well" and empty praise.
- **Middle**: the problem the seller solves and the result, in one or two sentences, with one proof point if it's real.
- **Ask**: one easy next step, such as a question they can answer in a line, or a short call with a clear purpose.
- **Close**: name, role, company, and an easy way to opt out.
- Aim for under 100 words. Read it aloud: would you send this to someone you respect?

## 4. Write the follow-ups

Write two or three, several working days apart. Each adds something new: a useful resource, a different angle, a short relevant example. The last one closes politely, such as "I'll leave it here". No guilt, and no "just bumping this".

## 5. Return the sequence

```
## Email 1, day 1
Subject: <subject>
<body>

## Follow-up 1, day <n> (same thread)
<body>

## Follow-up 2, day <n>
<body>

Personal details used: <line>, based on <source>
To confirm: <claims or facts to check>
```

## Rules

- Never invent a mutual contact, a past conversation, a customer result, a statistic or a compliment. Mark gaps "to confirm".
- No fake "Re:" or "Fwd:", no false deadlines, and no misleading subject lines.
- Follow the email-marketing and privacy laws where the recipient is. Some places require consent even for business addresses, and most require the sender's identity and a way to opt out. Honour opt-outs at once.
- Use only professional, public information. Leave the recipient's personal life out of it.

## Example

> Subject: Your second office
>
> I saw you're opening a second office and hiring for it. When teams grow that quickly, setting up new starters often takes longer than planned. We helped <customer, with permission> cut set-up time from <figure> to <figure>. Would a 15-minute call next week be useful? If not, just say and I won't write again.
