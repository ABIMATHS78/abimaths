# Proposal writer

Turn what was learnt in discovery into a clear proposal, in the seller's own format, that shows the buyer they were understood.

## 1. Get what you need

If anything below is missing, ask for it in one short message. Mark smaller gaps "to confirm" and carry on.
- the seller's proposal template, or a past proposal to follow;
- discovery notes: the buyer's situation, goals, measures of success and how they decide;
- the scope: deliverables, timeline, and what the buyer must provide;
- pricing and terms, from the seller only;
- case studies the seller may share, and who will read the proposal.

## 2. Follow their format

Use the seller's template, section order, headings and tone. If there's none, use the default structure below.

## 3. Lead with the buyer

Open with their situation and goals, in their own words. Then set out the recommended approach and why it suits them, and tie each outcome to how they'll measure success.

## 4. Be exact on scope and price

- State what's included, what isn't, the assumptions, and what the buyer must provide. Clear exclusions prevent most disputes.
- Copy prices exactly as given. Show options only if the seller offers them, with what each includes.
- State the currency, taxes, payment terms and how long the offer stands. Mark anything not given "to confirm".
- Check that every total adds up.

## 5. Finish and check

- Add proof that fits this buyer, and only proof the seller confirms they may share.
- Write the executive summary last: one page at most, for the reader who reads nothing else.
- Check names, dates and figures match throughout, and that no other client's name or text is left in.
- Put a list of every "to confirm" item at the top, for the seller.

## Default structure

```
1. Executive summary: their goal, our recommendation, investment, timeline
2. Your situation and goals
3. Our recommended approach
4. Scope: included, not included, assumptions, your responsibilities
5. Timeline and milestones
6. Investment: options, totals, payment terms, how long the offer stands
7. Why us: relevant proof
8. Next steps and how to accept
```

## Rules

- Never invent prices, discounts, terms, deliverables, dates, case studies or testimonials. Use the seller's figures, and mark gaps "to confirm".
- Don't reuse another client's details or confidential information.
- Take legal terms, such as liability and intellectual property, from the seller's own contract. Don't draft new legal commitments.
- If the work involves handling personal data, flag that a data-processing agreement may be needed under the data-protection law that applies.
- Write in plain language, and explain any acronym.

## Checklist

- [ ] Every price and total matches the seller's figures.
- [ ] Every goal from discovery is addressed.
- [ ] Exclusions and assumptions are stated.
- [ ] No other client's name or text remains.
