# Discovery call prep

Go into a discovery call ready to learn, with a clear agenda, good questions, and hypotheses you're prepared to be wrong about.

## 1. Get what you need

If anything below is missing, ask for it in one short message. Mark smaller gaps "to confirm" and carry on.
- who's attending, with their roles, and how the call came about;
- research on the company, or permission to do some;
- what the seller offers, and how long the call will be;
- the team's own qualification checklist, if it has one.

## 2. Set the goal

Write what the seller must learn to judge whether there's a fit and what the next step should be. Then write what the buyer gets from the call, such as a clearer view of their options. A call that only helps the seller rarely leads anywhere.

## 3. Form hypotheses

Write three to five guesses about their situation, their problem, what it costs them and why they might act now. Link each one to evidence from the research, and pair it with a question that would test it.

## 4. Plan the agenda

Keep it short, and share it at the start. Confirm the time available and ask what they want to cover. For a 30-minute call, for example: introductions and agenda (3 minutes), their situation and priorities (12), impact and how they decide (8), fit and next steps (5), spare (2).

## 5. Write the questions

Use open questions, one at a time, grouped by theme:
- **Situation**: only what the research couldn't answer.
- **Problem**: what's not working, how they handle it today, what they've tried.
- **Impact**: what the problem costs in time, money or risk, and who feels it.
- **Timing**: why now, and what happens if nothing changes.
- **Decision**: who else is involved, how they buy, how budgets are set.
- **Success**: what good would look like a year from now.

Have follow-ups ready, such as "Can you give me an example?" and "What makes that hard?".

## 6. Return the prep sheet

```
# Discovery call: <company>, <date>
Attendees: <names and roles>
Goal: <what we need to learn> | For them: <what they get from the call>
Agenda (<length>): <item> (<minutes>), <item> (<minutes>)

| Hypothesis | Evidence | Question to test it |
|---|---|---|

Questions: <grouped as above>
Their likely questions: <question>: <honest answer, or "to confirm">
Next step to propose: <step>, with <who>, by <when>
```

## Rules

- Never invent facts about the prospect. Label hypotheses clearly, and mark gaps "to confirm".
- Listen more than you talk, and let the answers change the plan.
- Don't promise features, prices or dates the seller hasn't confirmed.
- If the call will be recorded or transcribed, ask everyone's permission at the start. The law in many places requires it.
- Keep notes professional, and record only what the deal needs, as data-protection laws expect.
