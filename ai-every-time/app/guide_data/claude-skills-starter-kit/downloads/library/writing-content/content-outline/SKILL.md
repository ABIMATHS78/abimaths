# Content outline

Plan a long piece so the writing goes faster and the finished piece holds together.

## Before you start

You need the topic, the reader and what they already know, the purpose (to teach, persuade, inform or sell), the format and the total length. Ask for the source material, research and examples available, and the deadline. Ask for anything missing in one short message.

## Steps

1. **Define the reader**: who they are, what they already know, and the question they bring to the piece.
2. **Write the promise**: one sentence on what the reader will know or be able to do by the end.
3. **List the questions the reader will ask**, in the order they'll ask them. These become the sections.
4. **Give each section**:
   - a heading that says what the reader gets;
   - its main point, in one sentence;
   - the supporting points;
   - the evidence, example or visual it needs, and where that will come from;
   - a word count, so the sections add up to the total.
5. **Check the flow.** Each section should lead to the next. Move, merge or cut sections that repeat or wander from the promise.
6. **Plan the opening and the ending**: how the piece hooks the reader, and what they should do next.
7. **List the gaps**: missing evidence, interviews to do, data to find, and questions for experts.

## Output

```
Working title: <title>
Reader: <who, what they know, their question>
Promise: <one sentence>
Length: <total words>

1. <Heading> (<words>)
   Point: <one sentence>
   - <supporting point>
   Evidence: <what, and where from>

Opening: <the hook>
Ending: <the takeaway and next step>
Gaps to fill: <list>
```

## Rules

- Never invent sources, data or experts to fill the outline. List what's needed as a gap.
- Keep one promise. If the outline serves two different readers or purposes, suggest splitting it into two pieces.
- Base the structure on the reader's questions, not on everything the author knows.

## Checklist before you finish

- [ ] The promise fits in one sentence.
- [ ] Every section serves the promise and makes one main point.
- [ ] The word counts add up to the total length.
- [ ] Every gap in evidence is listed.
