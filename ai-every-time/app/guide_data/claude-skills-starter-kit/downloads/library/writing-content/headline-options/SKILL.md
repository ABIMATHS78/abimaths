# Headline options

Give the writer a strong set of headlines to choose from, each one honest about what the reader will get.

## Before you start

You need the piece itself, or a summary of it, and where the headline will appear: a web page, an email subject line, a social post or print. Ask who the reader is, any length limit, the brand's voice, and the search terms people use, if search matters. Ask for anything missing in one short message.

## Steps

1. **Find the core benefit**: what the reader gets from the piece, in plain words.
2. **Write ten to fifteen options across styles:**
   - direct: says exactly what the piece offers;
   - how-to: promises a method;
   - number: a list or a specific figure from the piece;
   - question: one the reader is already asking;
   - surprising: an unexpected finding from the piece;
   - specific outcome: a result, with a time frame or detail.
3. **Fit the place.** Keep email subject lines short enough to read on a phone, often under about 50 characters. Put the important words first, since long headlines get cut off. Include the search phrase naturally where search matters.
4. **Check every option against the piece.** Strike out any that promise more than the piece delivers.
5. **Pick the best three**, and say why each could work and who it's for.
6. **Suggest a test**, if the writer can compare two headlines with real readers.

## Output

```
Core benefit: <one sentence>

| # | Headline | Style | Characters | Delivers? |
|---|---|---|---|---|

Top three:
1. <headline>: <why>
2. <headline>: <why>
3. <headline>: <why>
```

## Rules

- **No clickbait.** Every headline must be true to the content. Don't invent figures, results or urgency.
- Don't use a statistic the piece doesn't contain.
- Avoid all capitals, piles of exclamation marks, and words that trigger spam filters in email subject lines.
- Count characters accurately, including spaces.

## Checklist before you finish

- [ ] Every option is true to the piece.
- [ ] Options cover several styles.
- [ ] Lengths fit where the headline will appear.
- [ ] The top three each have a reason.
