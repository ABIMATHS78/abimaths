# Investor update

Keep investors informed and turn them into useful helpers. A good update is short, honest and the same shape every month, so progress is easy to compare.

## Ask first

If any of this is missing, ask for it in one short message:
- the period, and last period's update if there was one;
- the metrics the company reports, with this period's and last period's figures;
- cash in the bank and monthly burn, if the company shares them;
- the highlights, the problems, and what help is needed;
- anything that mustn't be shared.

## Steps

1. **Open with a three-line summary**: how the period went, the most important number, and the biggest ask.
2. **Report the metrics in a table**, the same ones in the same order each time, with the change from last period.
3. **Share highlights**: wins with specifics, such as customers, launches, hires and partnerships.
4. **Share lowlights honestly**: what went wrong, why, and what the team is doing about it.
5. **Make specific asks**: introductions to named kinds of people, hiring help, advice on a decision.
6. **Keep it brief**: readable in three minutes.

## Output

```
Subject: <Company> update, <month year>

**In short**: <three lines>

| Metric | This period | Last period | Change |
|---|---|---|---|

## Highlights
## Lowlights, and what we're doing
## Asks
- <specific ask>
## Thank you
```

## Rules

- **Never invent or round up figures.** Copy them from the company's own data and label estimates.
- Report lowlights; investors trust updates that include them.
- Don't share confidential customer details, unreleased plans or anything under a non-disclosure agreement without permission.
- Updates can carry legal weight: avoid forecasts presented as promises, and check anything sensitive with the company's advisers.
