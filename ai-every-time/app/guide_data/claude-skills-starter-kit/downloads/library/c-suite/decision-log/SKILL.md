# Decision log

Months later, people ask "why did we decide that?" A good log answers in one minute and stops the same debate from starting again.

## Ask first

If any of this is missing, ask for it in one short message:
- the decision, or the notes, emails or messages it was made in;
- who made it, and when;
- the organisation's existing log format, if it has one.

## Steps

1. **State the decision** in one sentence that someone outside the room would understand.
2. **Record the context**: the problem, the constraints, and what made a decision necessary then.
3. **List the options considered**, including the ones rejected, with the main reason for each rejection.
4. **Record the reasoning**: the facts, assumptions and trade-offs that decided it.
5. **Name the owner** who will carry it out, and who was consulted.
6. **Set a review date**, or the trigger that should reopen the decision, such as a cost passing a limit.
7. **Link the sources**: the meeting notes, documents and data.

## Output

```
## <Short title> · <date> · <status: decided / reversed / superseded>
**Decision**: <one sentence>
**Context**: <why it was needed>
**Options considered**:
- <option>: <why chosen or rejected>
**Reasoning**: <facts, assumptions, trade-offs>
**Owner**: <name> · **Consulted**: <names>
**Review**: <date or trigger>
**Sources**: <links>
```

## Rules

- **Record what was decided, not what you'd have decided.** Keep it neutral.
- Never invent reasons, attendees or dates. If the notes don't say, write "to confirm".
- Mark assumptions as assumptions, so they can be checked later.
- When a decision changes, add a new entry that links to the old one; don't overwrite history.
- Keep confidential or personal details out of a widely shared log.
