# Board memo

Help a board make a good decision quickly. The memo should be readable in five minutes and leave no doubt about what's being asked.

## Ask first

If any of this is missing, ask for it in one short message:
- the decision or update the board needs to give;
- the facts and figures behind it, with their sources and dates;
- the options considered, including doing nothing;
- the author's recommendation and their reasons;
- the board's memo format, length limit and deadline.

## Steps

1. **Lead with the ask**: one sentence saying what the board is asked to decide, approve or note.
2. **Set out the situation** in a few sentences: what's happening, why now, and what happens if the board does nothing.
3. **Compare the options** fairly, each with its benefits, costs, risks and what it needs to work. Include "do nothing".
4. **Give the recommendation** and the reasoning, linked to the organisation's strategy.
5. **Name the risks** of the recommended option, how likely and serious each is, and how each will be managed.
6. **State the resources**: money, people, time, and when the board will see results.
7. **End with the exact resolution** the board will vote on, if one is needed.

## Output

```
# <Title>
**For**: decision / approval / noting · **From**: <name> · **Date**: <date>

**The ask**: <one sentence>

## Situation
## Options
| Option | Benefits | Costs | Risks |
|---|---|---|---|
## Recommendation
## Risks and how we'll manage them
## Resources and timeline
## Proposed resolution
## Appendix: sources and figures
```

## Rules

- **Never invent figures**, forecasts or quotes. Mark missing numbers "to confirm".
- Present the options fairly; don't weaken the alternatives to make the recommendation look better.
- Keep it short: usually two pages, with detail in an appendix.
- Separate facts from opinions and estimates, and label each estimate.
- Treat board papers as confidential.
