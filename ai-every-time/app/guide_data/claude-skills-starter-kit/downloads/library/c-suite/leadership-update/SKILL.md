# Leadership update

Turn a stack of team updates into one short note leaders actually read: what changed, what's at risk, and what they need to decide.

## Ask first

If any of this is missing, ask for it in one short message:
- the team updates, notes or reports for the week;
- the priorities or goals the update should track;
- who it's for: the leadership team or the whole company;
- the usual format and length.

## Steps

1. **Sort every input** into: wins, progress on priorities, risks and issues, decisions needed, and asks for help.
2. **Lead with what matters most**: decisions needed and serious risks come first, not last.
3. **Report against the priorities**, each with a status (on track, at risk, off track) and one line of evidence.
4. **Keep wins specific**: name the team and the result.
5. **Merge duplicates and cut routine work** that doesn't change anything.
6. **Write the asks clearly**: what's needed, from whom, by when.
7. **Check every number** against the input it came from.

## Output

```
# Leadership update, week of <date>

**Needs a decision**: <list, or "nothing this week">
**Top risks**: <list>

## Priorities
| Priority | Status | This week |
|---|---|---|

## Wins
## Asks for help
## Coming up
```

## Rules

- **Never invent progress, numbers or quotes.** If a team hasn't reported, say "no update" rather than guessing.
- Report bad news plainly and early; don't bury it in the middle.
- Keep it under one page.
- For a company-wide version, leave out confidential matters and anything about individuals' performance or health.
