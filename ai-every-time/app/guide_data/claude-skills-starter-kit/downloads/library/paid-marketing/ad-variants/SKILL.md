# Ad variants

Write ads that fit the format, say one clear thing each, and differ in a way the results can explain.

## 1. Get what you need

If anything below is missing, ask for it in one short message. Mark smaller gaps "to confirm" and carry on.
- the product or offer, and who it's for;
- the platforms and ad formats;
- the landing page the ads lead to, so the ads can match it;
- proof the advertiser can back up: real figures, reviews, awards, guarantees;
- the brand's voice, and any words or claims to avoid;
- how many variants they want, and the current best ad, if there is one.

## 2. Confirm the limits

List every field in each format (headlines, descriptions, main text, display path, button) with its character limit. If you can browse, check the platform's current guidance. If you can't, ask, or mark the limit "to confirm" and keep lines short. Never guess a limit.

## 3. Choose the angles

Give each variant one angle, so a winner tells you something. Useful angles:
- the main benefit, or the problem it removes;
- proof: a real figure, review or award;
- the offer or price;
- speed or ease;
- an objection answered, such as "no contract";
- who it's for, such as "for small dental practices".

Use only angles the landing page supports. An ad that promises what the page doesn't deliver wastes the click.

## 4. Write and count

- Lead with the angle, in the customer's words rather than the company's.
- Give each ad one clear call to action.
- Make the variants genuinely different, not the same line reworded.
- Count the characters in every field, spaces and punctuation included, and show the count against the limit. Rewrite anything over it.

## 5. Return the variants

```
Limits used: <platform and format> | <field> | <limit> | <checked, provided or "to confirm">

## Variant <n>: <angle>
Tests: <the question it answers, such as "Does price beat speed for this audience?">
<Field>: <text> (<count>/<limit>)
Matches the landing page: <yes, or what to change>

## Claims to confirm
- <claim>, and the evidence needed
```

## Rules

- Never invent results, ratings, reviews, prices, discounts or awards. Every claim needs evidence the advertiser holds. Mark gaps "to confirm".
- No false urgency or scarcity, such as "only 3 left", unless it's true.
- Follow the platform's ad policies and the advertising and consumer-protection laws where the ads run. Health, finance, housing, jobs, gambling, alcohol and politics often carry extra rules.
- Don't suggest you know the reader's personal situation, as in "Struggling with debt?". Many platforms' policies forbid it.
- Use a competitor's brand name only if the person confirms it's allowed.
