# Landing page review

Find what stops people who click an ad from taking the next step, and say what to fix first.

## 1. Get what you need

If anything below is missing, ask for it in one short message. Mark smaller gaps "to confirm" and carry on.
- the page: a link if you can browse, or the copy plus screenshots for mobile and desktop;
- the ads or keywords that send traffic to it;
- the one action the page wants visitors to take, and who those visitors are;
- current traffic and conversion rate, if known, and any limits on what can change.

## 2. Check message match

Put the ad next to the page. Does the headline repeat the ad's promise, offer and key words? Do the price, images and tone match? A visitor should know within seconds that they're in the right place.

## 3. Check clarity

Without scrolling on a phone, can a visitor tell what this is, who it's for, why it's better and what to do next? Look for one main action, a specific headline and plain words.

## 4. Check trust

Look for real proof near the point of decision: genuine reviews, client names used with permission, guarantees, certifications, contact details, and a short note on how form data will be used. Flag proof that's missing or can't be checked.

## 5. Find the blockers

- Forms that ask for more than they need.
- Vague buttons, hidden prices, or costs that appear only at the end.
- Distractions: menus, extra links, competing offers.
- Slow loading, broken elements on mobile, pop-ups that cover the content.
- Accessibility problems: weak colour contrast, small tap targets, unlabelled form fields.

Put broken things first, then high-impact, low-effort changes. Fix what's clearly broken, and suggest a test where the better option isn't obvious.

## Rules

- Base every point on what you've seen. Say which parts you couldn't see, such as the mobile view or the thank-you page.
- Never invent conversion rates, benchmarks or uplift figures. Rate impact as high, medium or low, and say why.
- Never suggest fake reviews, false scarcity, hidden costs, pre-ticked consent boxes or other tricks. Consumer-protection and data-protection laws in many places forbid them.
- Check that the page's claims can be backed up, that its forms explain how personal data will be used, and that cookie consent meets the rules where visitors are.
