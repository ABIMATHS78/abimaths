# Ad performance readout

Turn campaign data into a short readout: the result, what drove it, and where the next part of the budget should go.

## 1. Get what you need

If anything below is missing, ask for it in one short message. Mark smaller gaps "to confirm" and carry on.
- exports by campaign, and ideally by ad set and ad: spend, impressions, clicks, conversions and conversion value;
- the period, and the period to compare it with;
- the targets, such as cost per sale or return on ad spend;
- changes made during the period: budgets, bids, creative, pages, tracking;
- the business's own figures for the period, such as orders or leads in its own system.

## 2. Check the data

Confirm that currencies, time zones and date ranges match. Note each platform's attribution setting. Platforms count conversions differently, often claim the same sale, and rarely match the business's own records. Never add their conversions together as if each were unique.

## 3. Find what changed

Start with the topline against target and the previous period: spend, conversions, cost per conversion, value, and return on ad spend. Then break any change in cost per conversion into its three parts:
- **cost of reach** (cost per thousand impressions): competition, season, audience size;
- **click-through rate**: tired creative, weaker relevance, new placements;
- **conversion rate**: the page, the offer, the quality of the audience, or broken tracking.

Cost per conversion = cost per thousand impressions ÷ (1,000 × click-through rate × conversion rate), so whichever part moved explains the change. Then drill down by campaign, audience, placement, device and ad. Check frequency for signs of fatigue. A sudden drop to zero usually means tracking broke, not demand.

## 4. Recommend budget moves

- Move money towards campaigns below target cost that are held back by budget, and away from those above it.
- Judge the next part of the spend, not the average. Extra spend in a campaign usually performs worse than what it already gets.
- Move in steps, and check each platform's guidance so changes don't restart its learning.
- Don't act on a handful of conversions. Say when the numbers are too small to judge.

## 5. Return the readout

```
# Paid performance readout, <period>
Summary: <result against target, the main driver, the main recommendation>

| Campaign | Spend | Conversions | Cost per conversion | Value | Return | vs previous | vs target |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|

## What changed and why
- <change>: driven by <cost of reach, click-through or conversion rate>, because <evidence>. Confidence: <high, medium or low>

## Budget moves
| From | To | Amount | Why | What to watch |
|---|---|---|---|---|

## Data notes
- <attribution, tracking or data gaps>
```

## Rules

- Never invent figures or causes. Label explanations you can't prove as hypotheses, and mark gaps "to confirm".
- Say plainly when results are poor. Don't hide a weak campaign inside a good total.
- Use aggregated data only, with no personal data in the readout.
- Don't recommend targeting that breaks platform policy or the advertising, privacy or anti-discrimination laws where the ads run.
