# Appeal letter

Write an appeal that makes one clear, specific ask and gives the reader a true reason to act now.

## 1. Get what you need

If anything below is missing, ask for it in one short message. Mark smaller gaps "to confirm" and carry on.
- the cause, and the specific need right now;
- who it's going to (current, lapsed or new supporters) and how: letter, email or both;
- the ask amounts, and what each one really pays for;
- a story shared with consent;
- the deadline or reason for urgency, if there's a real one;
- how people give (link, form or phone), and who signs it.

## 2. Make one ask

Choose a single action, such as a gift today, and one way to respond. Everything in the appeal should support it.

## 3. Build the appeal

- **Opening**: a person, a moment or a problem, in concrete terms.
- **Need**: why it matters and why now. Use urgency only if it's real.
- **Solution**: what the organisation does, and how a gift helps.
- **Ask**: specific amounts, each linked to real costs, such as "<amount> pays for <item>".
- **Repeat**: ask again near the end, and in a P.S., which many readers see first.
- **Thanks**: warm and specific.

Write to one reader as "you", in short paragraphs and plain words, from a named person.

## 4. Adapt for the channel

For email, keep it shorter, put the ask and button near the top and again at the end, and write three subject lines and preview text. A letter can run a little longer, with a reply form.

## 5. Return the appeal

```
Subject lines (email): <three options>
Preview text: <text>

<Appeal, with [button: wording] or [reply form] marked>

P.S. <the ask, restated>

Notes
- Costings: <amount> = <what it buys>, from <budget, or "to confirm">
- Consent for the story: <status>
- If more is raised than needed: <what happens to the extra>
```

## Rules

- Never invent stories, costs, match funding, deadlines or results. Every "<amount> pays for <item>" must come from real costs. Mark gaps "to confirm".
- No false urgency, guilt or exaggerated need, and no words or images that strip people of dignity.
- Get consent for stories and photos, and anonymise where needed, with extra care for children and people at risk.
- Contact only people who can lawfully be contacted, respect opt-outs, and follow the fundraising and data-protection rules where supporters live. Include any details those rules require, such as the charity's registered name and number.
- If the appeal is for a specific purpose, say what happens to money raised beyond the target, so gifts are used as promised.
- Mention tax relief or match funding only if it's real and applies where the reader lives.
