# Donor update

Tell donors what their money did, specifically and honestly, so they feel informed and respected rather than sold to.

## 1. Get what you need

If anything below is missing, ask for it in one short message. Mark smaller gaps "to confirm" and carry on.
- who it's for (all supporters, regular givers or one major donor) and how it will be sent;
- the period it covers;
- what happened: activities, numbers, and a story shared with consent;
- what was hard, and how the organisation is responding;
- whether there's an ask, and who will sign it.

## 2. Lead with the impact

Open with the most meaningful result, in concrete terms: "38 families now have..." rather than "we made a difference". Use only figures from the data provided.

## 3. Make the donor the subject

Write "your support" more often than "we". Be accurate: if gifts were pooled, say "together, supporters like you made this possible". Report restricted gifts against the purpose the donor chose.

## 4. Tell one story

Choose one person or moment, told with consent and dignity, and show what changed for them. Avoid words or images that pity people or take away their agency.

## 5. Be honest about challenges

Say what didn't go to plan, why, what was learnt and what's changing. Donors trust organisations that tell them the hard parts too.

## 6. Look ahead and thank

Say what's next. Include an ask only if it fits, and make it clear. If there's no ask, say so, so the update reads as a thank you. End with a specific thank you from a named person.

## 7. Return the update

```
Subject or headline: <the specific impact>

<Opening: the main result, addressed to the donor>
<Story: one person or moment, with consent>
<What your support made possible: three to five specific results>
<What was hard, and what we're doing about it>
<What's next, and the ask if there is one>
<Thank you, from a named person>

Notes: <figures and their sources; consent status; anything "to confirm">
```

For a major donor, make it more personal and specific to their gift. For a wide audience, keep it short.

## Rules

- Never invent numbers, stories, quotes or results. Mark gaps "to confirm".
- Get informed consent for stories, names and photos. Change identifying details where needed, and take extra care with children and people at risk.
- Contact donors only in the ways they agreed to, and follow the fundraising and data-protection rules where they live.
- Don't claim a gift achieved more than it did, and don't hide bad news.
