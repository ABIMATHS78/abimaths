# Impact report

Build a report that shows honestly what a programme did and what changed as a result, and never confuses the two.

## 1. Get what you need

If anything below is missing, ask for it in one short message. Mark smaller gaps "to confirm" and carry on.
- the programme data: attendance, activities, survey results and records, all anonymised;
- the intended outcomes, or the theory of change;
- the reporting period, and who the report is for;
- the funder's reporting template, if there is one;
- stories, quotes and photos shared with consent, and a financial summary.

## 2. Check the data

Note the period, the definitions, missing data and survey response rates. Count unique people and attendances separately. Note how each outcome was measured: before and after, self-reported, or observed by someone else.

## 3. Sort every figure

- **Inputs**: money, staff and volunteer time.
- **Outputs**: what the programme did and who took part, such as sessions run and people reached.
- **Outcomes**: measured changes in knowledge, behaviour or circumstances.
- **Impact**: longer-term change the programme contributed to.

Label each figure. "500 people attended" is an output, not an outcome.

## 4. Report outcomes honestly

Give the measure, the number who answered, and the base: "Of 120 participants, 84 completed the follow-up survey, and 61 of them (73%) said..." Say when results are self-reported. Use "contributed to" unless the evidence shows the programme caused the change.

## 5. Compare and learn

Set results against targets (met, partly met or missed) with reasons. Then write what worked, what didn't and what will change. Use one or two stories to show the data in a real life, not to replace it.

## 6. Return the report

```
# <Programme> impact report, <period>
Summary: <who you worked with, the main outcomes, the main lesson>

## What we did (outputs)
| Output | Target | Achieved | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|

## What changed (outcomes)
| Outcome | How measured | Base | Result | Target | Status |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|

## Stories: <one or two, told with consent>
## What we learnt: <what worked, what didn't, what will change>
## Finances: <income and spend, or "to confirm">
## About the data: <sources, response rates, limits>
```

## Rules

- Never invent data, outcomes, quotes or stories. Mark gaps "to confirm".
- Show bases and response rates. Don't overstate small samples, and don't claim causes the evidence can't support.
- Protect participants: anonymise, don't report groups small enough to identify someone, and get consent for stories and photos, under the safeguarding and data-protection rules where the organisation works.
- Report shortfalls as clearly as successes.
