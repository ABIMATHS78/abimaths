# Funder research

Find funders that genuinely match a project, and rank them so effort goes where the chances are best.

## 1. Get what you need

If anything below is missing, ask for it in one short message. Mark smaller gaps "to confirm" and carry on.
- the project: what it does, who benefits, where, what it costs, and when;
- the organisation: legal form, registration, annual income, location, track record;
- how much is needed, and by when;
- funders already approached or already funding the work;
- funders to avoid, for example under an ethical policy.

## 2. Define the search

Set the themes, the people who benefit, the area, the type of funding (project, core or capital), the amount and the timeframe.

## 3. Search

If you can browse, use public grant databases, charity and company registers, funders' own guidelines, annual reports and accounts, and lists of past grants. Include community foundations, companies' giving programmes and public bodies. If you can't browse, say so, and give search terms and the kinds of source to check.

## 4. Check each funder

- **Priorities**: do they fund this kind of work, for these people, in this area?
- **Eligibility**: location, type of organisation, income limits, exclusions.
- **Grant size**: what they actually give, from past grants, not only the stated maximum.
- **Process**: open rounds and deadlines, or invitation only.
- **Track record**: past grants to similar organisations.

## 5. Rank by fit

Rate each funder strong, possible or weak, with reasons. Mark invitation-only funders for relationship building rather than an application.

## 6. Return the shortlist

```
## Search summary
<what was searched, where, and on what date>

| Funder | Fit | Priorities match | Eligible? | Typical grant | Deadline | Process | Source |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|

### <Funder>
Why it fits: <reason, with evidence>
Watch out for: <exclusions or conditions>
Next step: <action>, by <date>
Checked: <source and date>

## Ruled out
- <funder>: <reason>
```

## Rules

- Never invent funders, programmes, deadlines, grant sizes or contacts. Cite a source and the date checked for every detail.
- Funders change their criteria. Tell the person to confirm everything on the funder's own pages before applying.
- Don't guess eligibility. Mark it "to confirm", and suggest asking the funder.
- If a funder says it doesn't accept unsolicited applications, don't suggest sending one.
- Use only public, professional information about funders' staff and trustees, and follow data-protection rules.
- Note any ethical concerns about a funder, for the person to decide on.
