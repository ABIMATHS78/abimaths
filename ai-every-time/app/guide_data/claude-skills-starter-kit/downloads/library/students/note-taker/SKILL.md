# Note taker

Turn rough notes or a long transcript into notes a student can learn from: organised, accurate and ready to revise.

## Ask first

If any of this is missing, ask for it in one short message:
- the notes, transcript or slide text;
- the course and level;
- what the notes are for: an exam, an essay, or keeping up with the course;
- how many flashcards they want (suggest 10 to 15).

## Steps

1. **Read everything first.** Find the main topics and the order that makes them easiest to follow, which may not be the order they were spoken in.
2. **Structure the notes.** One heading per topic. Under each: the key idea in one sentence, then the details, examples, formulas or dates that support it.
3. **Define every key term** the first time it appears, in plain words and as the lecturer used it.
4. **Keep the lecturer's examples.** Exam questions often build on them.
5. **Flag what's unclear.** Where the transcript is garbled, a number looks wrong or the notes contradict themselves, mark it "check" and say what to check.
6. **Write the summary**: five to eight bullet points the student could explain from memory.
7. **Write the flashcards.** One idea per card. Mix the types: a term and its meaning, a question that needs a reason, a step in a process, and a sentence with a gap to fill.
8. **List questions for the lecturer or tutor**, based on the gaps you found.

## Output

```
# <Lecture title>, <date if given>

## Notes
### <Topic>
- Key idea: <one sentence>
- <details, examples, formulas>

## Key terms
- <term>: <plain meaning>

## Summary
- <five to eight points>

## Flashcards
| Front | Back |
|---|---|

## To check
- <unclear points and questions for the tutor>
```

## Rules

- Use only what's in the material. If you add context from elsewhere, label it "added, not from the lecture" and keep it short.
- Never invent figures, dates, quotes or references. Mark anything uncertain "to confirm".
- Leave out personal details from recordings, such as other students' names or comments.
- Notes are for the student's own study. Recording and sharing lectures is covered by the institution's rules and copyright law, so suggest they check before sharing.
- Notes support learning. Don't turn them into an essay or answers for assessed work.
