Your manager walks past and says "Write me a report on sales."

Then leaves.

You'd have 5 questions. Claude has the same 5. It just doesn't ask them. It guesses.

That's why the first answer feels average. It was written for nobody in particular.

The fix isn't a magic phrase. It's a brief. The two minutes you'd give a sharp new colleague:

1. The GOAL (what it's for)
Not "a report on sales".
"Help the board decide whether to keep selling in Ghana."

2. The BACKGROUND
"They meet Friday. They've seen last quarter's numbers. Two members want to pull out."

3. The MATERIAL
Paste or attach it. Notes, numbers, the draft.
If Claude can't see it, it invents it.

4. The SHAPE
"One page. Three options, each with its cost & main risk. Recommendation at the top."

5. The FINISH LINE
"Done when every number comes from the sheet. If anything's missing, list it instead of guessing."

Don't know what to put in it? Flip it:
"Before you start, ask me the five questions you most need answered to do this well. Wait for my answers."
(The questions are often better than the brief you'd have written.)

Two habits that cut three rounds of fixes to one:
→ Show it a good example. One past report beats ten adjectives.
→ Correct it like a colleague's draft. Say what to change AND why: "Shorter, because the board reads it on their phones."
