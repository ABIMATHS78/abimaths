# LinkedIn voice guide

Learned from 26 example posts and 26 graphics (trained 2026-11-23). Use it to write new posts in the same shape, in the author's own voice.

## Voice
- Teach one practical thing per post. The reader should be able to act on it today.
- Talk to one reader as 'you'. Use 'I' only for experience the author actually gave you.
- Short lines, one idea per line, a blank line between blocks. Most lines stay under 60 characters.
- Be concrete: exact menu paths (Settings → Capabilities), prompts to copy in quotes, real numbers, time boxes.
- Hold a clear, slightly contrarian opinion and say it plainly. No hedging and no hype words.
- Casual rhythm: '&' for 'and' in short lines, a few parenthetical asides ('(for free)', '(trust me)'), and at most one to three words in CAPS for emphasis.
- Make the post worth saving: steps, lists, prompts to copy, and a graphic that sums up the whole post.
- Close with one clear next step: save it, send it to a specific kind of person, or follow a link (one from the brief, or the author's website).

## Measured shape of the example posts
- Length: 2,038 characters typical (most between 1,269 and 2,807). LinkedIn's limit is 3,000.
- Lines: 30 typical (most between 22 and 58) non-empty lines; average line 56 characters; 70% of lines are 60 characters or less.
- Hook (first one or two lines): 116 characters typical (most between 38 and 142). LinkedIn cuts off around 210 characters on desktop and 140 on phones, so the promise must land before that.
- 50% of hooks end with a colon that opens the list; 54% contain a number; 12% put one word in CAPS; 31% have a parenthetical aside.
- 88% use a numbered or 'Step N' list; 69% use glyph bullets (most used: >, ☑, →, ✦, -, ☑️); 42% include a prompt to copy in quotes; 58% use → menu paths; 69% use '&'.
- They talk to 'you' about 2.2 times as often as about 'I'. 19% use emoji (usually 0 per post); 0% use hashtags.

## Body structures
- `numbered_steps`: 'Step 1.' to 'Step N.', one to three short lines each; near the end a twist that turns the trick into the real lesson, then that lesson in one line.
- `labelled_sections`: Headed blocks such as 'LESSON 1:', 'Level 1.' or '→ LEVEL 1 - The basics.', two to five lines each.
- `tip_list`: Numbered tips ('1.' or '1)'), each a bold-sounding first line plus one or two lines of why or how.
- `link_library`: Guide titles with links, optionally grouped by level, then the save / share / get-more triple.
- `old_new`: 'Old: ...' / 'New: ...' pairs (or 'BAD PROMPT:' / 'BETTER PROMPT:') that show the fix, not just describe it.
- `delete_keep`: 'First, delete these:' as a long numbered list, then 'Now keep only these N things:' as a short one.
- `avoid_fix`: N things to stop doing, each with a one-line jab, then N fixes.
- `category_sections`: Numbered CAPS section titles ('1. AI + SALES'), each with ✦ sub-steps, separated by '-----'.
- `rapid_fire`: Every line starts with '> ', grouped in short blocks: a whole masterclass in one-liners.
- `decision_steps`: 'Step 1: [question]?' then '☑️ No: ...' / '☑️ Yes: ...', each answer with a prompt example.
- `glossary`: Grouped '☑ Term: short definition' lines under small numbered headings.
- `stats_then_opinion`: Three numbered facts from the brief, then a short opinion and what to do about it.

## Closings
- `save_share_more`: Three numbered asks: bookmark it (the ⋯ menu), ♻️ repost it for one person who needs it, and where to find more: [a link from the brief, or the author's site].
- `save_this`: Bookmark this. You'll want it the next time you [task].
- `share_with_someone`: Share this with someone who [specific trait] (but still [specific gap]).
- `repost_for_team`: ♻️ Repost it for the colleague who still [specific pain].
- `ps_guide_link`: PS: the full step-by-step, with every prompt, is here: [link] (only if the brief gives that link)
- `link_gate`: The full [prompt/checklist] has [N] parts. It was too long for this caption, so it's here: [link] (only if the brief gives the link to the full version)
- `mission`: One line on why the author shares this (their mission, from the voice notes), then where to find more: [site].
- `anaphora_close`: Three short 'I could tell you [exaggeration].' lines, then 'But I won't.' and a plain promise. Write fresh wording every time.
- `closing_instruction`: So close LinkedIn. Open [tool]. Try one [specific task] today.
- `authority_quote`: End on a short quote from a named expert, with their name and role. (only if the quote and source are in the brief)

## Formatting
- LinkedIn shows plain text: no **bold**, no # headings, no markdown links. Paste-ready text only.
- Line break after every one or two sentences; a blank line between blocks.
- Bullet glyphs: ✦ for sub-points under a section, ☑ for checklists, → for sub-items and menu paths, > for rapid-fire lines, '1.' or 'Step 1.' for steps.
- Optional separators between big sections: '-----' or '___'.
- Prompts to copy go on their own lines in straight double quotes, with [square-bracket placeholders].
- Menu paths use arrows: Settings → Capabilities → Memory.
- Emoji are rare: ♻️ in a repost line, ⚠️ for a warning, 👉 before a link. Three at most.
- No hashtags unless the brief asks for them.

## Content rules (always)
- Use only facts, numbers, names, quotes, stories and links the brief gives you. If a point needs a fact you don't have, write around it or put [CHECK: what's needed] so the author fills it in.
- Never reuse facts, product claims, links or numbers from the example posts: they belong to another creator and may be out of date.
- Never mention the example posts' creator, their newsletter, their sites or their short links. Point readers only to the author's own site.
- Never teach cheating (having an AI complete quizzes, courses or certifications, or 'fooling' recruiters) and never fake proof.
- No engagement bait ('Comment YES', 'Like if you agree'), no invented urgency, no fake screenshots of real people.
- Write new sentences. Borrow the shape of a hook or a list, never the wording of an example post.

## Words and habits that make a post read as AI-written: never use them
- "delve", "crucial", "pivotal", "tapestry", "foundational", "here's the thing", "hope this helps", "after careful consideration", "provide a quick update", "most people", "robust", "seamless", "realm", "landscape", "game-changer", "game changer", "in today's fast-paced world", "let that sink in", "unlock", "elevate", "supercharge", "navigate the", "ever-evolving", "testament to", "it's important to note".
- No em dashes (—). Use a colon, a comma or a new line.
- No 'It's not X, it's Y' sentences, no 'X quietly does Y', no grand pronouncements.

## Graphics
- One graphic per post. Portrait 4:5 (1080 × 1350) unless the template needs another shape.
- The graphic stands on its own: someone who only sees the image should still learn the thing and want to save it.
- Headline of 2 to 8 words that echoes the hook's promise, with one accent word.
- Scannable text only: item lines of about 4 to 12 words, no paragraphs, numbers and verbs up front.
- The engine adds the footer (author credit and site); don't put credits inside the content.
- Motion (GIF or MP4) when the graphic builds step by step: mind maps, roadmaps, decision trees, kinetic text. Static for dense cheat sheets meant to be saved.
- Shapes seen: portrait 4:5 (24), landscape (2). 19% of the graphics are dark.
- Recurring backgrounds and inks: #fcfcfe, #e0dfdd, #cec4bf, #000000, #afadac, #7b7b7c.
- Recurring accent colours: #db795a, #eb1918, #e8ccbf, #774737, #c8907e, #af6850, #afb066, #9c824a.

### Which template fits
- `cheatsheet`: Many sub-topics, each with steps, tables or a prompt: the whole guide on one page.
- `link_list`: A library of guides, links or one-line tips (styles: pinned, notes, terminal, doc).
- `prompt_ladder`: A bad, better and best version of the same prompt.
- `prompt_anatomy`: One prompt, coloured by what to delete, keep and add.
- `split`: Two opposing lists: delete vs keep, myth vs fact, before vs after.
- `funnel`: Things to avoid narrowing down to what to do instead.
- `glossary`: Terms and short definitions (12 to 100).
- `roadmap`: A path of steps or use cases from start to finish.
- `cycle`: A set of 5 to 12 items around one big central number, like a curriculum.
- `mindmap`: One topic with 4 to 8 branches. 'whiteboard' style animates like a hand drawing.
- `decision_tree`: Pick between four options with two yes/no questions.
- `card_grid`: N tools, skills or ideas, each with a name, a tag, one line and an example; or feature explainers with labelled facets.
- `big_number`: A comparison of 2 to 6 real numbers with their source.
- `badge`: One bold title on a shape: an announcement or a single idea.
- `bookshelf`: A reading list of 5 to 12 guides drawn as book spines.
- `kinetic`: A short motion video: one idea per scene, at most 8 words on screen.
- `screenshot_callout`: Stickers and highlight boxes on a screenshot the author supplies.
